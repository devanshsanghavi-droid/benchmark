# R07 Fares, pay, platform rules and regulation: simulation variables for RoboTaxi Bench

Domain R07 of 12. Owner of the full list: Devansh. Researched on 19 Oct 2026 for the "independent variables list" action from the RoboTaxi Bench meeting.

## Summary

This domain sets the money rules: what a rider pays, how much of it reaches the driver or fleet, and which rules limit what an agent may do. Most of it can be taken straight from law: the NYC taxi meter ($3.00 plus $0.70 per fifth of a mile or per slow minute), every taxi surcharge and flat fare, the taxi lease caps, the hail rules, the hours cap and the Uber/Lyft minimum-pay formula are all in the Rules of the City of New York, read here from a verbatim copy current through rules effective 13 Aug 2026. The biggest levers on profit per hour are the platform take rate (NYC drivers got about 79% of the base fare on average in 2025), the TLC pay floor (about $1.24 per passenger mile plus $0.63-0.69 per passenger minute since the 1 Aug 2025 reset [uncertain: derived from the rule text, not from the TLC's posted CPI-adjusted rates, which could not be read; see R07-025/026]), surge, and for taxis the lease cost of $105-129 per 12-hour shift. Also large are the JFK $70 flat fare, which hands the traffic risk to the taxi driver, street-hail legality by vehicle type and zone, airport queue rules, and what a driver may see before accepting. Smaller but real effects include the rush, night and LaGuardia surcharges, the $0.75 / $1.50 congestion-zone trip fee, rider-side taxes that change demand but never reach the driver, cancellation fees, quests, WAV premiums, fines and licence costs. The rules also block two shortcuts an agent might try: taking long routes on purpose, and refusing taxi riders by destination. Robotaxis need their own rule pack: NYC has no commercial pathway (testing permits expired 31 Mar 2026 and state law requires a hand on the wheel), so a NYC robotaxi track is a declared counterfactual priced like an FHV or like a Waymo city. Many numbers change every year (pay-floor CPI each 1 March, Green Rides targets each January, toll phase-ins), so the simulator has to version its rules by date.

### How to read the tables

- **Variable**: ID, name, then (priority; applies to; component).
- **Impact**: effect on driver or fleet profit per hour (high / medium / low).
- **Role**: world = outside input; rule = designer-set rule or price; agent = model decision; state = changes during run; setting = benchmark knob.
- **Agent sees?**: visible / public_or_forecast / hidden (simulator only).
- **Source tags**: [P] primary read directly; [P*] primary via a verbatim copy; [S] search snippet or secondary; [U] unverified background knowledge; [design] our suggestion or calculation.
- Driver types: human app driver (Uber/Lyft, NYC "HVFHV"), taxi (yellow/green, street hail plus e-hail), robotaxi (Waymo-like). Levels: single car and fleet of N.

### Research limits (read before trusting numbers)

- The shared web-search budget for this run was already used up when this domain started, so **no web searches were made**. nyc.gov, mta.info, uber.com, waymo.com, the CPUC, news sites and most other hosts were blocked or unreachable from this session.
- The rule text comes from **jehiah/nyc_code_archive**, a structured verbatim export of the American Legal Publishing XML of the Rules of the City of New York and the Administrative Code. Its manifest says "Current through rules effective August 13, 2026" (generated 31 Aug 2026). It is tagged [P*].
- The HV-FHV expense factors, factor history, licence costs and insurance premiums come from a verbatim GitHub text copy of the Center for New York City Affairs report "Revised Expense Model for the NYC TLC HV-FHV Minimum Pay Standard" (Feb 2025), tagged [P*]. TLC data-dictionary field text (document dated 18 Mar 2025) comes from verbatim excerpts in a GitHub knowledge base, also [P*].
- The CPI-adjusted per-minute rate actually posted by the TLC for 2025-26 could not be read. The $0.63-0.69/min figure is **derived**: the indexed factor ($0.338 at Mar 2024, plus 1-2 CPI steps) divided by the 53.3% utilization set in the rule. App fee levels, surge, quests and cancellation fees have no public NYC source and are marked [U] or [design].
- Fact-check of 19 Oct 2026: every cited RCNY and Admin Code section was re-read from the archive, and the GitHub secondary sources were re-read; corrections are marked in place and summarised in the Fact-check log at the end.

## Key interactions with other domains

- **R10 Traffic**: the meter switches from distance to time at 12 mph (R07-001, R10-043). The NYC app floor pays per passenger minute (R07-026), so slow traffic is partly paid for app drivers. The JFK $70 flat fare (R07-011) makes airport-corridor traffic risk (R10-010) fall entirely on the taxi driver. The shortest-route rule (R07-052) limits route choice (R10-042), and floor pay counts actual miles and minutes. Toll amounts live in R10-021; who pays them lives here (R07-018, R07-038).
- **R05 Riders and demand**: every rider-side fee (R07-005 to R07-009, R07-023), surge (R07-020) and the robotaxi premium (R07-061) feed price elasticity (R05-020) and robotaxi uptake (R05-027). Tip behaviour (R05-047/048) uses the pass-through rules (R07-037). Cancellations and no-shows (R05-022/037) trigger fees (R07-035, R07-036). Airport demand (R05-061 to R05-064) meets the airport rules (R07-051) and flat fares.
- **R06 Competing supply and matching**: surge (R07-020) depends on idle supply. The lockout rules (R07-030) and licence cap (R07-057) set how many competitor cars exist. Platform take (R07-024) and the floor shape the pay offers that competitor drivers accept.
- **R09 Driver as a person**: the hours cap (R07-047 = R09-005), refusal rules (R07-042 / R09-032), acceptance and cancellation consequences (R07-043/044 / R09-040), quests (R07-033 / R09-025), lockouts (R07-030 / R09-042), deactivation (R07-045 / R09-043) and the off-duty "returning home" exception (R07-050 / R09-020). These rows overlap and should be merged at synthesis: R07 keeps the rule values and fines, and R09 keeps the behaviour.
- **R08 Vehicle and operating costs**: lease caps and add-ons (R07-016/017), licence fees (R07-055), insurance (R07-056) and the Green Rides EV/WAV mandate (R07-057) are cost or eligibility inputs to R08's cost ledger.
- **R02 Time and calendar**: rush and night windows, legal holidays (R07-003/004), CRZ peak hours, and rule effective dates (R07-028, R07-065).
- **R01 Geography**: polygons for the 96th St congestion area, the 60th St CRZ, the hail exclusionary zone (Manhattan south of E 96th / W 110th plus airports), airport zones 1/132/138, the city boundary for out-of-city fares and pay, and robotaxi ODD polygons (R07-060).
- **R03 Events and R04 Weather**: events and rain push up surge (R07-020). Severe weather can pause robotaxi service (R07-060), and incidents can suspend AV permits (R07-063).
- **R11 Fleet**: cruising cap (R07-032), utilization standards (R07-031), operator licence fees (R07-058), Green Rides quota (R07-057), partner revenue split (R07-062) and ODD (R07-060) all apply at fleet level.
- **R12 Benchmark settings**: the rule vintage (R07-065), the profit accounting (R07-066) and the city rule pack (R07-067) are harness settings. R12 should also decide how much of the rulebook the agent is told, for example whether it knows the pay-floor formula or the surge map.

Key corrections:
1. R07-045: NYC now has a just-cause deactivation law for Uber/Lyft drivers (Admin Code 20-1281 to 20-1290, Local Law 52 of 2026, effective 28 Jul 2026). It requires a 30-day probation, progressive discipline, 14 days' notice and reverse-seniority economic layoffs, and it counts restrictions of 72 h+ in a row or 168 h+ per year as deactivation. The dossier had said no such law existed.
2. R07-002 and the worked example: the rule prices the initial unit at $3.00 and each *additional* unit at $0.70.
