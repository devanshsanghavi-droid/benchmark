## Summary

1. **Lyft's offer card in most US markets (upfront-pay markets)** shows the following before the driver accepts:
   - the **Upfront Driver Fare**: estimated earnings, which include any bonus attached to that ride, but not tips, tolls or multi-ride bonuses;
   - **pickup and drop-off locations** on a map with the full route;
   - the **estimated time and distance** of the ride;
   - since 8 Oct 2024, an **estimated $/hour rate** for the ride, counting only pickup and on-trip time.

   The pay is fixed on acceptance unless the trip "significantly" changes, which Lyft says happens on about 4% of rides. Upfront pay applies to Standard, Extra Comfort, Black, XL and Black SUV rides. [S, official Lyft Help and Hub; Driver Addendum last updated 9 Feb 2026]
2. **History:**
   - Before 2022, destination and trip length were a perk. From Oct 2019, Lyft Rewards Gold and Platinum drivers (at least 90% acceptance rate and a 4.9 rating) saw trip length and direction.
   - In Oct 2022 Lyft tested full upfront pay and destination in 18 US cities. By 14 Jun 2023 it covered "most regions across the country".
   - The 2024 pay commitments added a per-ride breakdown and a weekly summary of rider payment, Lyft fee and external fees (6 Feb 2024), and a 70%-of-rider-payments weekly floor (20 markets in Feb 2024, all US in May 2024).
   - On **1 May 2026** a **30% monthly cap on the Lyft fee** replaced that floor. [S]
3. **NYC is the big exception, and it matters for RoboTaxi Bench:**
   - Lyft's pay guide says **upfront pay is not available in New York City, Washington state or Portland**. Drivers there are paid per minute and per mile, and in NYC at least the TLC per-trip minimum.
   - **From 1 Jul 2024, NYC Lyft drivers see the "upfront trip details"** (destination, distance, time, full route) **only while their acceptance rate is 85% or higher**. The rate is measured over their last 100 requests.
   - The Oct 2024 features, including the $/hour estimate, are not available in NYC, Portland or Washington state.

   So a NYC Lyft driver who declines too often loses the destination. This dynamic link between declining and information is worth modelling. [S, official Lyft Help] [corrected by fact-check: this rests on one undated Help-page snippet; that it is still in force in Oct 2026 is unverified. It is company policy: NYC rules set no pre-acceptance disclosure duty for app drivers (35 RCNY 59B/59D and Admin Code 19-540 to 19-549 searched verbatim by the Uber fact-check, P*). Whether the NYC card shows any pay figure is unknown.]
4. **Declining has costs:**
   - **Lyft Rewards points:** 1x below 50% acceptance, up to 2x at 90-100%. Colorado drivers are exempt.
   - **NYC trip details:** hidden below 85% acceptance (point 3).
   - **Airport FIFO place:** lost after one decline under the general rule, or after 3 at some airports.
   - **Queued rides:** auto-accept is the default.

   No source shows Lyft deactivating drivers for a low acceptance rate. California's Prop 22 bars requiring drivers to accept specific requests as a condition of access. [S]
5. **While idle**, drivers see:
   - Turbo (scheduled, 10-40% more per ride) and Flash Turbo (real-time map zones) bonuses, which stack since Oct 2025, plus Ride Challenges;
   - the airport FIFO queue, with hour-by-hour wait guidance at major airports from fall 2026;
   - location filters (Destination mode and similar), limited to **2 uses per day**;
   - an earnings dashboard, the weekly summary, and AI tools (Earnings Assistant in 2025, Lyft Advisor in fall 2026).

   Prime Time as a fare multiplier has given way to flat bonuses and zones. [S]
6. **Not shown before accepting:**
   - the rider's fare and Lyft's cut for the ride (both appear only after the trip);
   - the rider's tipping history and punctuality (a Sept 2025 test was cancelled on about 10 Oct 2025 after rider backlash);
   - tolls and tips;
   - the pricing formula, and whether other drivers declined the same ride;
   - in NYC, Washington state and Portland, any upfront fare;
   - in NYC below 85% acceptance, the destination, time and distance.
7. **How Lyft differs from Uber, briefly:**
   - Lyft shows a **$/hour estimate** on the card; one 2024-25 blog says Uber did not.
   - Lyft has no Trip Radar equivalent confirmed for 2025-26. Its "ride chooser" existed only in "select regions" in 2022-23.
   - Lyft caps its own fee at 30% a month (2026).
   - Lyft's NYC gate is acceptance-based (85%).
   - Both companies show pay and destination in most US markets (Uber also since 2022, with surge included in the shown pay).
   - Both use a 100-request acceptance-rate window and a limit of 2 destination filters per day.

**Implication for the benchmark (design note, not evidence).** A realistic "Lyft-like, non-NYC" observation is: upfront pay (with any per-ride bonus folded in), $/hr estimate, pickup point and probably pickup ETA and distance, drop-off point and route, trip time and distance, ride type, stops, and probably rider rating. A realistic "Lyft-in-NYC" observation is: no upfront pay (pay follows the TLC per-minute/per-mile floor or above), with destination, time and distance shown only while the agent's rolling 100-request acceptance rate is at least 85%.
