Strand: regulation, NYC in 2026, taxis and robotaxis for contrast, and evidence on how offer information changes driver behaviour. ## Summary

1. **In NYC, no law requires Uber or Lyft to show drivers anything before they accept** [P\*]. I searched all of 35 RCNY ch. 59B and 59D, Admin Code 19-540 to 19-549, and the 2026 wrongful-deactivation law (Admin Code 20-1281 to 20-1290, in force 28 Jul 2026), and none of them sets such a duty. The only pre-booking disclosure duties run to the **rider**: a binding quote, or a fare no more than 120% of the estimate (59B-23; Admin Code 19-545). [corrected by fact-check: the binding quote in 59B-23 applies to livery bases only. For HVFHVs (Uber, Lyft) 59B-23 requires a fare estimate on request, "inclusive of all fees and any price multiplier", and caps the charge at 120% of it (or of the top of a range no wider than 150%); Admin Code 19-545 (L.L. 2016/049, in force 19 Aug 2016) sets the same cap for black-car bases. No NYC rule or law sets an offer timeout in seconds either [P\*].]

   NYC does impose a pre-acceptance disclosure duty on **food delivery apps**. Admin Code 20-1521(d) (L.L. 2021/114, in force 22 Apr 2022; amended by L.L. 2025/123, in force 26 Jan 2026) requires them to disclose before the worker accepts:
   - the pickup address(es);
   - the estimated time and distance from first pickup to final drop-off;
   - the customer's tip;
   - the pay, excluding the tip.

   [corrected by fact-check: the drop-off address itself is not on the list, only the time and distance to the final drop-off; as amended by L.L. 2025/123, 20-1521(d) covers any "delivery service" and "contracted delivery worker", while the distance, bridge and tunnel settings in (a)-(c) cover food delivery and courier services [P\*].]

   Workers can also set a maximum trip distance and avoid chosen bridges and tunnels. So the Council has legislated this kind of disclosure, just not for ride-hail drivers.
2. **What NYC app drivers actually see in 2026 differs by company** (app practice comes from the sibling strands):
   - **Lyft NYC:** no upfront pay. Pay follows per-minute and per-mile rates at or above the TLC floor. Since 1 Jul 2024 a driver sees the "upfront trip details" (destination, distance, time, route) only while their acceptance rate over the last 100 requests is 85% or higher [S, official Lyft Help via lyft.md]. [uncertain: one undated Help snippet; the Lyft dossier's fact-check lowered it to medium, and whether the gate survives the Aug 2025 TLC access rules and the Jul 2026 deactivation law is unconfirmed.]
   - **Uber NYC:** almost certainly shows the upfront fare and drop-off, as elsewhere in the US. However, no NYC-specific source was found. Uber's NYC page says earnings "meet or exceed the TLC minimum", and the new Uber Pro programme excludes NYC [S via uber.md; NYC display U].
3. **NYC rules shape the value of the offer information more than its content:**
   - The pay floor makes pay almost a fixed function of trip miles and minutes. The 59D-22 formula sets the in-city rates; trips that leave the City earn double rates for the miles and minutes outside it [P\*]. [corrected by fact-check: the out-of-city rates ($1.700/mi, $0.574/min) are double the base factors but are not divided by the utilisation rate, so they are about 1.37 times the effective in-city per-mile rate and about 1.07 times the per-minute rate, not double (assuming they are CPI-indexed in step) [P\*, own arithmetic].]
   - In the TLC Jan 2023 data, a linear fit on miles and minutes explains **90% (Uber) and 95% (Lyft)** of the variance in per-trip driver pay [P, own calculation]. The fitted slopes ($1.44/mi and $0.49/min for Uber) are close to the floor formula.
   - Even so, pay per mile varies widely from trip to trip: p10 to p90 is $2.56 to $7.96 for Uber and $2.53 to $6.84 for Lyft. That is because short trips carry the fixed part and minutes count heavily in traffic.
   - So in NYC, a driver who sees the destination and the trip time can already estimate pay well. A displayed pay figure adds mainly the surge and bonus tail: the top 10% of Uber trips pay at least 1.33 times the fitted value.
4. **NYC lockouts and what drivers saw:**
   - **Before the new rules:** platforms restricted log-ins to keep utilisation high under the floor formula. Bloomberg (10 Oct 2024) found lockouts nearly every hour, based on more than 5,300 crowdsourced screenshots [S]. Uber paused them in Sep 2024 under a deal with the city [S]. [uncertain: the Lyft dossier dates the city's lockout agreement to Jul 2024; the exact pause date was not re-checked.]
   - **Since 1 Aug 2025 (35 RCNY 59D-22(c)):**
     - a platform must give 72 hours' notice before restricting a driver on a calendar day;
     - once online, a driver keeps access for at least 16 hours, and the window restarts after 8 hours offline;
     - a platform may still restrict a driver at once for "excessive cancellation or non-acceptance of dispatches" [P\*]. [corrected by fact-check: the no-notice exceptions in 59D-22(c)(3) also cover missing licensing credentials, TLC rule violations and other platform rule violations, including misconduct [P\*].]
   - **Since 28 Jul 2026:** a restriction of 72 hours in a row, or 168 hours in a year, counts as a "deactivation". It then needs just cause and 14 days' notice (Admin Code 20-1281, 20-1282) [P\*].

   For the benchmark, declining is legal, but heavy declining can lawfully cost a driver access without notice. At Lyft NYC it also costs the destination display.
5. **Taxis (yellow and green), street hail:**
   - The driver learns nothing about the trip until the rider is seated, and may not try to. 35 RCNY 80-20(a)(4) bars any "attempt to learn the destination ... before the Passenger is seated", even if the driver then takes the trip. The exceptions are Flex Fare e-hail trips and a logged-off driver heading home or to the garage under 80-19(a)(5) or (b)(6).
   - The driver must go to any destination in NYC, Nassau, Westchester or Newark Airport, subject to 15 listed justifications. One of them: more than 8 hours of driving lets a driver refuse Newark, Nassau or Westchester.
   - At airport stands with both Short Haul and Long Haul lines, the dispatcher's ticket fixes the trip class: a Short Haul ticket may not take a Long Haul rider, and the reverse (80-19(e)(3)). [corrected by fact-check: this applies only "if there are Taxicabs available in both lines" [P\*].]

   All [P\*], rules current through 13 Aug 2026.
6. **Taxi e-hail (Curb, Arro and other TLC-licensed apps, including Uber and Lyft taxi listings that run through them):**
   - By rule the driver app "may only disclose to a Driver a potential Passenger's pickup location, drop-off location, and fare estimate, or binding upfront fare quote if it is a Flex Fare Trip". The rider's trip ID or username may follow only after the driver accepts (35 RCNY 78-21(a)(3)).
   - E-hail use is optional, and the driver can opt out while on duty (78-21(a)(4); 80-19(a)(1)). But once the driver accepts, refusing the rider is a refusal violation (80-20(a)(5)).
   - Accessible requests must be shown "in a way that does not indicate ... that the Passenger will require additional assistance" (78-21(a)(7)(ii)).
   - The app must be velocity-gated while the car moves; accept, cancel and "arrived" are allowed as single taps (78-21(a)(2)).

   All [P\*].
7. **Robotaxis (Waymo-style):**
   - There is no driver to choose. The fleet dispatcher receives the rider's full request before assigning a car: pickup, destination (the rider must enter one) and the quoted fare. It can decline a request only by policy, for example outside the operating area or when no car is available [U].
   - On partner platforms (Waymo on Uber in Phoenix, Austin and Atlanta; Waymo with Lyft in Nashville, 2026) the platform routes eligible requests to the AV fleet [U/S, R07 and R09 notes].
   - NYC has no commercial robotaxi service. The testing permits expired on 31 Mar 2026 [S, R07-059].
8. **Laws elsewhere that do require pre-acceptance disclosure:**

   | Market | Law | What the driver must see before accepting | In force | Source |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Colorado | SB24-075 | Destination, expected pay, expected distance and time, and an estimate of what the rider will be charged; a single post-trip screen with rider paid, driver paid, tip and take rate | 1 Feb 2025 | [S] |
   | Minnesota | Minn. Stat. 181C.02 | Time and miles to pickup, trip time and miles, and total pay before tips, with "sufficient time" to review | Statute 2024; pay floor from 1 Dec 2024; disclosure date not confirmed | [S] |
   | Massachusetts | AG settlement, 27 Jun 2024 | Pre-acceptance transparency including pay | 2024 | [S]; exact list unverified |

   **No disclosure duty:**
   - **California:** Prop 22 (in force 16 Dec 2020) has none; it only makes "no forced acceptance" a condition of contractor status (Bus. & Prof. Code 7451(b)) [S/U].
   - **Washington (HB 2076, pay from 1 Jan 2023)** and **Seattle:** their TNC laws set pay floors and receipts. I could not confirm a pre-acceptance display duty [U]. Lyft offers no upfront pay in WA, NYC or Portland, all per-minute/per-mile floor markets [S].
   - **Illinois and Chicago:** no enacted rule found [U].
9. **Outside the US:**
   - **EU:** the Platform Work Directive (EU) 2024/2831 (in force 1 Dec 2024; transposition due 2 Dec 2026) requires information on automated monitoring and decision systems: what they decide and the main parameters. It does not require trip-level disclosure at offer time [U].
   - **UK:** no statute. The UK Supreme Court in *Uber BV v Aslam* (19 Feb 2021) treated withholding the destination until pickup, and acceptance-rate monitoring, as signs of Uber's control over drivers [U]. Uber UK has shown upfront prices and destinations since about 2020-21 [S via uber.md].
   - **France:** the Cour de cassation (4 Mar 2020) made a similar point about the unknown destination [U].
10. **Evidence on behaviour:**
    - **Cherry-picking:** showing the destination and pay produces it. Uber said about a third of California drivers declined more than 80% of requests under the 2020 AB5 regime. This is a company claim made during a policy fight [S via uber.md]. Uber's India destination pilot (2022) reduced cancellations [S].
    - **Acceptance drivers (stated choice):** pickup time is the strongest negative factor, and surge and long trips raise acceptance (Ashkrof et al. 2022; a Seattle study) [S via R09].
    - **Algorithmic pay:** Dubal (2023, *Columbia Law Review*) argues that opaque, personalised per-offer pay ("algorithmic wage discrimination") makes earnings unpredictable [U].
    - **Take rates:** reports link the move to upfront pay with higher platform take: about 32% rising to about 42% for Uber, 2022-2024 (NELP, Jul 2025) [S via uber.md]. An Oxford and Worker Info Exchange study of UK drivers (2025) reports a similar finding [U].
    - **NYC pay dispersion** is measured above (point 3).
11. **Driver heuristics** (reported, not official):
    - accept on a dollars-per-mile threshold, commonly quoted at $1 to $2 per mile, with $1 a mile as the older floor;
    - or on dollars per minute (about $0.30 to $0.50) or dollars per hour;
    - decline long pickups for short trips, and outbound trips to low-demand areas that mean an unpaid return;
    - use destination filters and run two apps at once to pick the better offer;
    - in Lyft NYC, keep acceptance at 85% or above to keep the destination visible.

    All [U] or [S]; no 2025-2026 survey was readable.

**For the benchmark (design note, not evidence).** The observation set should be a scenario setting, not one fixed schema:

| Regime | What the agent sees before deciding | Rules that apply |
|---|---|---|
| (a) US upfront (Uber-like, CO/MN-compliant) | Pay, pickup ETA and distance, trip time and distance, drop-off zone | — |
| (b) Lyft-NYC | No pay; destination, time and distance only while rolling acceptance is at least 85% | — |
| (c) Taxi street hail | Nothing before the rider is seated | Must accept any legal destination |
| (d) Taxi e-hail | Pickup, drop-off, and a fare estimate or Flex quote | Optional to accept; no refusal after accepting |
| (e) Robotaxi fleet | Everything the rider entered | Assignment instead of accept or decline |

In NYC regimes, the TLC floor makes pay largely inferable from time and distance. Add a lockout rule under which excessive non-acceptance can trigger an immediate access restriction (59D-22(c)(3)).

**Key corrections.**
1. **Rider quotes (59B-23).** The *binding* quote is a livery-base duty only. Uber and Lyft (HVFHVs) must give a fare estimate on request, "inclusive of all fees and any price multiplier", and may charge at most 120% of it.
2. **No NYC offer timeout.** No NYC text sets an offer timeout in seconds. The finding that NYC imposes no driver-side pre-acceptance disclosure duty is re-confirmed.
3. **Delivery-worker disclosure (Admin Code 20-1521(d)).** The drop-off address is not required, only the time and distance to the final drop-off. As amended (L.L. 2025/123, in force 26 Jan 2026), (d) covers all "delivery services".
4. **Pay floor, per-minute rate.** The $0.287 in 59D-22(a)(2) is the 2019 base, which is CPI-indexed. "$0.287 / 53.3%" therefore understates the floor. The 2026 [S] values ($1.283/mi, $0.681/min) are consistent with yearly indexing.
5. **Pay floor, out-of-city rates.** They are not divided by utilisation, so they are about 1.37 times the in-city per-mile floor and about 1.07 times the per-minute floor, **not double**.
6. **Lockout exceptions (59D-22(c)(3)).** Restriction without notice is also allowed for licensing, TLC-rule and platform-rule violations, including misconduct, not only for non-acceptance or cancellation.
7. **Deactivation law (L.L. 2026/052, enacted 29 Jan 2026, in force 28 Jul 2026).** It also sets a 30-day probation period, requires 120 days' notice for economic layoffs, and requires progressive discipline under a written policy.
8. **Flex Fare date.** The only amendment shared by all five sections that mention it (58-26, 78-21, 80-17, 80-20, 80-25) is the one in force **21 Sep 2024**. The codified driver-display clause in 78-21 cannot date from Mar 2023. A 2023 pilot is unconfirmed.
9. **US offer card.** Pay and destination are **not** shown in all US markets. Lyft shows no upfront pay in NYC, WA or Portland, and in NYC shows the destination only at 85% acceptance or above. Uber says "most" US cities. Tips and tolls are excluded; Uber includes surge, and Lyft includes any ride bonus. Uber's window is about 10-15 s, longer in some markets from Oct 2025. No US law sets seconds. Confidence lowered from high to medium.
10. **Lyft NYC 85% gate.** Confidence lowered from high to medium: one undated snippet, and current status unconfirmed.
11. **Taxi destination.** "Never shown before seating" holds only for street hails. E-hail apps may show the drop-off before acceptance (78-21(a)(3)).
12. **Airport lines (80-19(e)(3)).** The Short Haul and Long Haul separation applies only when taxis are waiting in both lines.
