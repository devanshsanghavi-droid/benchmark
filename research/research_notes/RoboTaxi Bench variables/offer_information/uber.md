# Uber driver app: what drivers see before, during and after a trip offer

Strand: Uber driver app (US first, with major non-US differences). Written 2026-10-21 for RoboTaxi Bench rows R06-025, R06-026 and R07-041 (all three were marked [U] for app practice).

## Summary

1. **The short answer (US, Oct 2026): yes, in most US markets a driver sees the pay, the destination and the trip time and distance before accepting.** A standard ride offer card shows:
   - the **upfront fare**, which is the driver's estimated earnings, not what the rider pays;
   - **pickup ETA and distance**, for example "2 min (0.3 mi)";
   - **trip duration and distance**, for example "10 min (4.9 mi) trip";
   - the **pickup and drop-off locations**;
   - the **rider's star rating**, plus a **Verified** badge since Sep 2024;
   - the **product type** (UberX, Comfort, XL, Black; Teen label);
   - whether the offer is **Exclusive** (an Accept button) or a shared **Trip Radar** offer (a Match button);
   - details on **extra stops**, expanded in the Oct 2025 redesign.

   Uber's own US blog post of 5 Mar 2025 says drivers see "the estimated fare and the pickup and drop-off locations" before accepting. Uber no longer publishes per-mile or per-minute rates, and drivers cannot opt out [S]. [uncertain: "most" US markets, not all. Uber's driver-basics snippet says "in most cities", and no source lists which US markets, if any, still lack upfront fares or destination; the "nationwide by about 2023" claim rests on one driver blog.]
2. **What the shown fare includes.** It includes surge. It excludes tips, tolls, other surcharges, wait-time fees and cancellation fees, which are added after the trip [S, Uber help snippet]. Tips are never shown before a ride. Uber Eats differs: its offers include the expected tip, and a 2026 tip guarantee applies there.
3. **Destination precision is not settled.** It may be an exact address, a street or an approximate area. An LA driver in Dec 2019 described "approximate pickup and drop-off locations". A Minneapolis driver in 2022 described the "destination address". No 2025-2026 source settles this.
4. **Rider name: probably not before accepting.** Uber says drivers see only "a rider's first name, star rating, verified rider badge, and trip details", without saying when. Several sources say the name is confirmed at pickup.
5. **Response window and declines.**
   - **Window:** about 10 to 15 seconds. An older Uber blog says 10-20 s, and an Uber page says 15 s. From Oct 2025 the window grew "by up to a third" in some markets (one LA test: 15 s, up from about 10 s) [S].
   - **Acceptance rate:** declining or letting an **Exclusive** request time out lowers it. The rate is measured over the last 100 Exclusive requests. **Trip Radar declines do not count** (Uber article dated 15 Jul 2025) [S].
   - **Account effects:** low acceptance does not cause permanent deactivation. It can cause short blocks, and missing 3 requests in a row auto-logs the driver offline (2017 rule).
   - **Where the rate matters:** airport FIFO queue place, which is lost after several declines in a row, and Uber Pro tiers. Under the 2026 Pro redesign Gold has no acceptance threshold, Platinum needs 25% and Diamond 70% (the 25%/70% figures come from the California version of the redesign, 20 Apr 2026).
6. **History.**
   - Before 2017: the driver saw no destination.
   - Nov 2017: a "Long Trip" (45+ min) flag.
   - Jul 2019: Uber Pro Gold and above could see trip duration and direction if their acceptance rate was at least 85%.
   - **Dec 2019, California (AB5 response):** destination and fare estimate shown, and drivers could decline without penalty. After Prop 22 (Nov 2020), Uber removed the driver price-setting multiplier in Apr 2021 and reportedly gated the destination behind acceptance.
   - **Early 2022:** "Upfront Fares" piloted in about 24 US cities, including Minneapolis.
   - **29 Jul 2022:** Uber announced expansion "to most of the US over the coming months". The Rideshare Guy says it went nationwide about 18 months after launch, so roughly 2023.
   - Trip Radar came to drivers in 2022 and was relaunched in the US on 15 May 2024. At select airports since then, all waiting-lot requests go through Trip Radar. [corrected by fact-check: US drivers had Trip Radar by early 2022 (TechAeris, 3 Mar 2022, cited in this dossier); the 21 Jul 2022 date comes from Swiss and Polish Uber posts, not the US launch.]
7. **NYC.**
   - **No law requires disclosure:** I found no TLC rule or NYC local law that requires showing HVFHV drivers the destination or pay before they accept [P\*].
   - **Lockouts:** TLC rules allow a platform to restrict a driver for "excessive cancellation or non-acceptance of dispatches" without the usual 72-hour notice (35 RCNY 59D-22) [P\*]. [corrected by fact-check: the exact cite is 35 RCNY 59D-22(c)(3). The section was last amended by City Record 2 Jul 2025 (effective 1 Aug 2025) and again on 22 Sep 2025 (effective that day).]
   - **Limit on long lockouts (added by fact-check):** NYC Local Law 2026/052, enacted 29 Jan 2026 and effective 28 Jul 2026 (Admin Code 20-1281 to 20-1290), treats any restriction that lasts 72 hours in a row, or adds up to 168 hours in a year, as a "deactivation". A deactivation needs just cause, including "a fair and objective investigation" (20-1282). So acceptance-based lockouts in NYC must stay short [P\*].
   - **Refusal rule (added by fact-check):** 35 RCNY 80-20(a)(1) applies to all TLC drivers, FHV included. It bars refusing "to take a Passenger ... to any destination within the City of New York, the counties of Westchester or Nassau, or Newark Airport" unless there are justifiable grounds. The rule against asking the destination in advance, 80-20(a)(4), covers only taxis and street-hail liveries [P\*]. [uncertain: whether declining an app offer before accepting counts as a "refusal" under 80-20; no TLC guidance was read. Refusing after accepting, for example at pickup once the destination is confirmed, plainly falls under it.]
   - **Pay floor:** Uber's NYC page says earnings meet or exceed the TLC minimum [S].
   - **Uber Pro:** the new program's terms list "All US markets except New York City" [S].
   - **Delayed Ride Guarantee:** excluded in NYC [S].
   - **Still unconfirmed:** no NYC-specific source confirms that the NYC offer card shows the destination and upfront pay. It very likely does, because Uber describes the feature as US-wide [U/S].
8. **States that mandate disclosure.**
   - **Colorado** (SB24-075, effective 1 Feb 2025): destination and expected pay before acceptance, plus expected total distance and time. After the trip, one screen must show the rider's total, the driver's share, the tip and the take rate. A court refused Uber's injunction on 31 Jan 2025, and Uber later settled.
   - **Minnesota** (2024 law, per the state labour department page): time and distance to pickup, trip time and distance, and total pay before tips, with "enough time to review". [uncertain: the effective date of the disclosure duty; probably 1 Dec 2024 with the pay floor [U], but the statute text (Minn. Stat. ch. 181C) could not be read.]
9. **What drivers do not see before accepting:**
   - what the rider pays, and Uber's cut of that trip (shown only afterwards in trip details, or on one screen in Colorado);
   - the tip;
   - the rider's full identity;
   - how many other drivers are shown a Trip Radar offer, and how the winner is picked (Uber says "the driver in the best position", not the first to tap Match);
   - any fixed rate card.

**For the benchmark:** model an Uber-2026-US offer with these fields:

`{product, exclusive|broadcast, upfront_driver_pay (incl. surge, excl. tips/tolls/wait), pickup_eta_min, pickup_mi, trip_min, trip_mi, dropoff_location (zone-level unless verified), rider_rating, verified_flag, n_stops, response_window ~15 s}`

Hide `rider_fare`, `take` and `tip`. Use a "blind" variant for pre-2019 and pre-2022 scenarios: pickup ETA, rating, product and surge zone only.

## Major non-US differences (brief)

- **UK:** upfront pricing has run since Oct 2020. The driver offer card shows the upfront price, excluding estimated tolls and fees, which are paid as incurred. A 28 Jan 2023 update added pickup distance and destination busyness to pricing [S, Uber UK blog].
- **India:** the destination has been shown before accepting since Jul 2022, after a pilot in 20 cities that at first required meeting an acceptance threshold. The payment method (cash or online) is shown too [S].
- **South Africa:** net earnings (fare minus service fee) and the destination have been shown since 29 May 2023 [S].
- **Canada:** "upfront offers" show expected take-home pay and the destination. Tips, wait fees and cancellation fees are added after the trip [S]. Ontario coverage criticises the opaque algorithm (Globe and Mail).
- **Romania (2023):** the offer shows net fare and pickup and drop-off locations. Trip details later show the rider's payment and amounts paid to third parties [S].
- **France (2022):** VTC drivers see price and destination, announced with Trip Radar. Uber Taxi in Paris shows the price estimate, distance and exact pickup and drop-off [S].
- **Latin America (Chile, Argentina, Mexico):** per-hour and per-km earnings badge pilots from 2025 [S].
- **Australia and New Zealand (2019):** the Pro "trip duration and direction" perk applied [S].

**Key corrections.**
1. **35 RCNY 59D-22 cite and history.** The rule is 59D-22(c)(3). Amended City Record 2 Jul 2025 (effective 1 Aug 2025) and 22 Sep 2025.
2. **New NYC limit on lockouts.** L.L. 2026/052 (enacted 29 Jan 2026, effective 28 Jul 2026) makes a restriction of 72 h in a row, or 168 h a year, a "deactivation" that needs just cause. Acceptance-based lockouts in NYC must therefore be short.
3. **New NYC refusal rule.** 35 RCNY 80-20(a)(1) bars any TLC driver, FHV included, from refusing a passenger over the destination within NYC, Westchester, Nassau or Newark Airport. Whether this covers declining an app offer before accepting is unresolved.
4. **Trip Radar dates.** US drivers had it by early 2022. 21 Jul 2022 is the Swiss and Polish date.
5. **Pickup ETA.** It is long-standing and also belongs in the pre-upfront "blind" offer. Colorado's text says only "total distance and time".
6. **What the shown fare includes.** Confidence lowered to medium, because the US exclusion of tolls and tips rests on one undated help snippet.
7. **NYC pay floor.** The rule text implies about $1.241/mi and $0.538/min before CPI adjustments. Uber's $0.681/min for 2026 is unverified.

**Not changed, still unverified [S]:**
- destination and pay shown in all US markets, as opposed to "most";
- the response window of 10-20 s, extended by up to a third in some markets from Oct 2025, with the NYC value unknown;
- the surge included and tips and tolls excluded on US cards;
- the Pro thresholds;
- the Colorado and Minnesota statute details.
