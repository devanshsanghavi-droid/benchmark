## 1. Summary

This domain covers the human behind the wheel: shift timing and length, fatigue and legal hour caps, breaks (meals, bathroom, prayer), home location and the 'take me home' end of shift, income and hours targets, experience, safety worries, preferences, other jobs, ratings and platform standing. For robotaxis it covers what replaces all of this: depot returns, cleaning, remote assistance and recovery. It also lists every decision the agent can take: 21 agent variables, 19 of them in section J and two (shift start and end) in section A.

The variables that move profit per hour most are the agent's spatial-temporal choices: when to work (R09-001/002), where to wait or reposition (R09-052/053), which offers to accept (R09-054) and whether to join an airport queue (R09-057). For robotaxis the big one is the duty cycle (R09-044), since a car with no driver can stay in service for about 20 hours a day.

Human limits matter mainly as constraints and as honest costs. NYC caps passenger time at 10 h per 24 h and 60 h per week [P*, upgraded by fact-check: 35 RCNY 80-14(f)], and TLC data showed a 23.8% higher injury-crash rate above 12 h/day [S]. Meals, bathroom stops (only 42 of 109 NYC relief stands are open to app drivers [S]) and prayer each cost minutes that hurt most when they land on a demand peak.

Labour-economics evidence is mixed on income targeting. Camerer et al. (1997) found negative wage elasticities, but Farber (2015, all 2009-13 NYC trips) found mostly positive ones, and Thakral & To (2021) found that recent earnings raise stopping [S]. We suggest treating targets as an optional persona and as a probe: does the LLM itself quit early on good days?

Experience is worth roughly 5-14% per hour: NYC taxi novices start about 5% behind and catch up in about 100-120 shifts, and Uber drivers past 2,500 trips earn about 14% more than those under 100 [S]. In the benchmark this maps directly onto the planned 10-day memory and learning test (R09-030, R09-068).

To keep 'work forever' from being optimal, the score needs a value of time (R09-023) and an explicit cost for the trip home (R09-020). Both are design settings, not facts.

One data gap stands out. The current TLC public yellow and HVFHV files carry no driver or medallion ID [P\*] (green likewise [P, fact-check: the green 2019-10 trip-file header has no driver or medallion field]), so per-driver shift behaviour must be calibrated from the 2009-13 FOIL-era trip data, Chicago's taxi data (which has a stable Taxi ID) or the published studies.

## 3. Decision list at a glance (role = agent)

These are the actions the model can take. Each is a row in the tables above; the list is here so the harness team can turn it into a tool schema.

- **R09-001 Shift start time and place**: When and where the driver goes online for the day.
- **R09-002 Shift end and total hours (daily, weekly)**: How long the driver works each day and how many days each week.
- **R09-052 Wait, cruise or park**: After a drop-off, stay put, cruise for street hails, or park at a staging spot to wait for app offers.
- **R09-053 Reposition to a target zone**: Drive empty to another zone expected to be busier.
- **R09-054 Accept or decline an offer**: Take or skip an app trip offer given pickup time, pay, destination and rider rating.
- **R09-055 Cancel after accepting, and no-show wait**: Cancel an accepted trip, or decide how long to wait for a rider who does not appear.
- **R09-056 Platform choice and multi-apping**: Which apps to be online on, and which offer to take when several arrive.
- **R09-057 Airport queue: join, stay or leave**: Whether to drive to JFK, LGA or EWR and wait in the queue for a long airport fare.
- **R09-058 Take a break (when, what, where)**: Choose the timing, type (meal, bathroom, prayer, rest) and place of breaks.
- **R09-059 Refuel or charge (when and where)**: Choose when and where to refuel or charge, trading downtime against range and price.
- **R09-060 Go home and use the destination filter**: Decide when to head home and whether to spend a destination-filter use to earn on the way.
- **R09-061 Route choice**: Pick the route for a trip or reposition: fastest, shortest, or avoiding tolls and the CBD charge.
- **R09-062 Chase surge or not**: Drive toward an area showing surge pricing.
- **R09-063 Street-hail choices and the off-duty light (taxi)**: Which hailing passengers to stop for and when to switch on the off-duty light, within anti-refusal rules.
- **R09-064 Rider interaction and service style**: How the agent responds to riders: greeting, small talk, help with bags, music, route requests, complaints.
- **R09-065 Rate or block riders**: Rate riders and optionally avoid being matched with them again.
- **R09-066 Ride-type opt-ins**: Opt in or out of shared rides, wheelchair-accessible requests, scheduled rides or larger and premium tiers.
- **R09-067 Information gathering**: Whether to spend steps or tool calls checking events, weather, news and traffic before deciding.
- **R09-068 Write and use memory notes**: What the agent records about hotspots, timing and mistakes for later days.
- **R09-069 Robotaxi fleet-ops decisions**: Send a car to the depot, clean it, charge it, take it out of service or hold it in reserve.

## 4. Key interactions with other domains

- R02 Time: break timing (R09-010, R09-058) should fall in demand troughs, so its cost depends on the R02 demand curve. The 4-5 pm taxi shift change (R09-003) creates a supply dip that R02/R06 must replay. Friday prayers and the Ramadan calendar (R09-013) are calendar-driven.
- R03 Events: whether event information helps depends on R09-067 (information gathering) and R09-030 (memory). Stuck robotaxis (R09-050) spike during blackouts and big events. Airport queues (R09-057) are fed by flight arrivals and disruptions.
- R04 Weather: rain raises demand and slows traffic, which changes the value of each extra hour (R09-023) and the stopping decision (R09-002). Farber's 'taxi in the rain' paper sits at this junction. Heat and cold could raise fatigue (R09-007) [design].
- R05 Riders: driver rating (R09-039), tips, no-show waits (R09-055), mess events (R09-046) and rider-interaction scoring (R09-064) all draw from the rider model. Rider ratings of drivers and driver ratings of riders (R09-065) are two sides of one mechanism.
- R06 Competing supply: simulated competitor drivers need R09's human parameters (shift start, hours and income targets, experience, home location) so that supply ebbs and flows realistically. Multi-apping (R09-056) and lockouts (R09-042) link drivers to platform supply management.
- R07 Fares, pay and rules: the TLC hour cap (R09-005), destination-filter quota (R09-017), Uber Pro thresholds (R09-040/041), service-refusal fines (R09-032), cancellation and no-show fees (R09-055), quests (R09-025) and the NYC utilization-based pay floor behind the lockouts (R09-042) are R07 rule values that R09 behaviour responds to.
- R08 Vehicle and energy: refuel/charge timing (R09-059) is best combined with meal breaks; depot charging capacity (R09-045) limits the robotaxi duty cycle (R09-044); taxi lease and garage (R09-003, R09-019) tie the shift to the vehicle. Personal costs (R09-037) are kept separate from vehicle costs.
- R10 Traffic and routing: commute deadhead (R09-016), route choice (R09-061), navigation skill (R09-028), driving speed (R09-029) and fatigue slow-down (R09-009) all act through R10 travel times.
- R11 Fleet of N cars: with human drivers each car has its own home, fatigue, hour caps and break needs, so a fleet manager must stagger breaks and plan end-of-shift positions. With robotaxis these become depot, cleaning and remote-assistance queues (R09-045 to R09-049) shared across the fleet.
- R12 Benchmark settings: persona choice (R09-004), value of time (R09-023), home-ending value (R09-020), memory (R09-030) and the information level (R09-067) are experiment settings. They need fixed defaults so results are comparable across models.
- Within R09: fatigue (R09-007) links hours (R09-002), breaks (R09-058) and crash risk (R09-008); acceptance behaviour (R09-054) feeds acceptance rate (R09-040), which feeds airport queue place (R09-057) and lockout priority (R09-042); home location (R09-015) drives the destination-filter decision (R09-060) and the end-of-shift score (R09-020).

## 6. Open questions

1. Should the LLM driver be bound by human-body constraints at all? We suggest two personas scored separately: 'robot driver' (no fatigue, meals or bathroom) and 'human driver' (timers enforced). Mixing them in one leaderboard would confuse what is being measured.
2. How to calibrate shift behaviour without driver IDs: the current TLC files have none. Options are the 2010-13 FOIL-era data (check whether ICPSR 37254 keeps hashed IDs), Chicago's taxi IDs, or published moments (Farber 2015, Haggag et al. 2017). Which is acceptable under the 'public data only' rule?
3. Fatigue-to-crash dose response: the only NYC number found is a threshold (+23.8% injury-crash rate above 12 h/day). Is a smooth curve worth building, or is a step at 10-12 h enough?
4. Current platform rules need re-checking against primary sources: Uber's 12 h/6 h offline rule and Lyft's 14 h rule are 2018 reports; destination-filter limits (Uber 2/day, Lyft 2-6) come from blogs; Uber Pro thresholds differ by city and year.
5. Are NYC app lockouts still active in 2025-26? Sources show a 2024 deal to end Uber's lockouts; a 2023 newsletter says there were none that year. The default for R09-042 depends on this. [answered by fact-check: since 1 Aug 2025, 35 RCNY 59D-22(c) requires 72 h notice and at least 16 h of access per session (P*); the 2026 default should be 'no in-shift lockouts'.]
6. NYC rating deactivation threshold: Uber says it is city-specific and does not publish it. We need a default (e.g. 4.6) clearly marked [design].
7. Robotaxi remote-assistance intervention rate and cleaning cadence are not disclosed by Waymo; only Cruise 2023 figures (a session every 2.5-5 miles) and fleet-level staffing (about 70 agents for about 3,000 cars) are public.
8. Not verified because the search budget ran out: Farber 2005 (stopping tied to hours), Crawford & Meng 2011 (hours and income targets), the University of Washington Seattle study (time targets matter more than earnings targets), and Waymo's NYC testing-permit requirement for a safety specialist. [partly answered by fact-check: 34 RCNY 4-17(d)(4) requires a test vehicle operator in the driver's seat (P*); Farber 2005 matches background knowledge (U); the other two remain unverified.]
9. Bathroom and prayer detour times: no public data. A small driver survey or Chicago taxi gap analysis could give break-duration distributions.
10. Should income targeting be imposed on the agent (realism) or measured in it (a bias probe)? The second is more novel: log whether the LLM stops earlier on high-earning days, chases surges that have ended, or stays in an airport queue past the break-even point (sunk cost).
11. How should 'take me home' be scored: as a hard end location, a cost per minute of final deadhead, or a bonus? The choice changes optimal end-of-shift strategy and should be fixed before model comparisons.

**Key corrections**
1. R09-042 lockouts: since 1 Aug 2025, 35 RCNY 59D-22(c) requires 72 h notice and at least 16 h of access per session, so the 2026 default is 'no in-shift lockouts'. Impact lowered from high to medium.
2. R09-051 safety operator: [U] replaced by rule text (34 RCNY 4-17(d)(4), eff. 7 Sep 2021). Waymo's NYC test permit expired on 31 Mar 2026, so a NYC robotaxi is a counterfactual. Impact raised from medium to high (it adds a full wage when on).
3. R09-003 and R09-019: the late-return fee is no longer [U]. It is capped at $25 per hour and at one shift's lease (58-21(c)(5)(iv)).
4. R09-005: the claim that a final over-limit trip 'counts toward the weekly total' is not in the rule. The rule bars double-counting a daily violation toward the weekly limit, and it resets the 10 h count after 8 consecutive hours with no passengers.
5. R09-017: taxis do have a 'take me home' rule. A logged-off driver returning to the garage or home may take riders going directly along the route (80-19(a)(5), 80-20(a)(4)(i)).
6. R09-032 and R09-063: refusal penalties completed: $350 / $700 on a guilty plea, revocation on a third offence within 36 months, and an exemption for Newark, Nassau and Westchester trips after 8 h of operation (80-20(b)(8)).
7. R09-061: the 2010 TLC case (1.8 million trips) was meter-rate fraud, not route detours. Taxis must take the shortest reasonable route (80-16(a)).
8. R09-016: the cruising cap was phased in (36% from Feb 2020, 31% from Aug 2020), is measured per company, and was reportedly annulled by a court in Dec 2019 [U].
9. R09-002: the Hall & Krueger hours claim was reframed (about half of US Uber drivers drove 1-15 h/week in late 2014, pooled across markets) [U].
