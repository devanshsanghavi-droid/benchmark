# R10 Traffic, travel time and routing: RoboTaxi Bench variables

Domain owner: R10 researcher. Default city: New York City. Covers a human app driver (Uber/Lyft, NYC HVFHV), a yellow/green taxi, and a robotaxi (Waymo-like), at single-car and fleet level. Source tags: [P] primary read directly, [P*] primary via verbatim copy, [S] search snippet or secondary, [U] unverified background knowledge, [design] our suggestion.

## Summary

Traffic is the denominator of every earnings number in RoboTaxi Bench: at Midtown speeds of about 4-5 mph (2017-2019) a car finishes far fewer paid trips per hour than on the same streets overnight, so the travel-time model effectively sets the whole economy of the simulation. The largest levers are a time-dependent speed profile (by zone, road class and hour of week), the gap between the slow core and the faster rest of the city, bridge and tunnel chokepoints, and the step change from congestion pricing on 5 January 2025 (CBD speeds up about 15% against comparison cities; morning-peak crossing times 4-36% shorter), which means each scenario must be calibrated on trip data from the same era it replays. Medium effects come from travel-time noise and correlated slow days, pickup and drop-off dwell, curb friction and GPS pin errors, routing objective and toll choice, and the quality of ETAs and traffic information the agent is shown. Small effects include NYC traffic rules (25 mph default limit, the right-on-red ban, busways, bus-lane and camera fines, anti-idling) and navigation slips; these matter mostly for realism and for robotaxi-versus-human contrasts. Deadhead is where agent skill shows (NYC app riders wait about 4.4-6.6 minutes on average; about 41% of FHV time in Manhattan below 96th Street was without a passenger in 2019), so empty moves must use the same engine as paid trips. Robotaxis need their own routing profile: Waymo only began freeway rides in November 2025, and a 2024 Los Angeles test found Waymo trips took about 2.2 times as long as Uber or Lyft. For the engine we recommend OSM routing (Valhalla's five-minute weekly speed profiles, or hourly OSRM speed files) for geometry and the live map, with edge times scaled so zone-pair x hour medians match TLC trip durations, because raw router defaults (OSRM assumes 65 km/h on untagged primary roads and 0.8 x the posted limit elsewhere, with only 2 s per traffic signal) underestimate Manhattan travel times several-fold. Evidence caveat: nyc.gov, the open-data portals, NBER and arXiv were unreachable from this sandbox and the shared web-search budget ran out mid-research, so most figures are tagged [S] (search snippet) and should be re-read from the primary source before they are hard-coded. A fact-check on 19 Oct 2026 re-tested the key numbers on real TLC trip records (HVFHV January 2023, yellow taxi 2023) and on verbatim NYC rule text; corrections are marked inline and summarised in the fact-check log at the end.

## Key interactions with other domains

- R01 Geography x R10: R01 supplies the road graph, taxi zones and land use; R10 adds the dynamic layer on top (speeds by hour, turn costs, time-conditional access such as busways and car-free park drives).
- R02 Time x R10: the hour-of-week and calendar index from R02 selects the speed profile; the midday lunch peak coincides with peak delivery double-parking (R10-038), and Gridlock Alert Days (R10-004) are calendar items.
- R03 Events x R10: events create closures and egress surges (R10-027; UNGA week Midtown under 4 mph); the event-information level (R12) should be crossed with the traffic-information level (R10-045) so the benchmark can separate the value of each.
- R04 Weather x R10: rain and snow cut speeds and raise crash rates (R10-006, R10-025) at the same time as they raise demand (R05); per-minute pay (R07) partly offsets slower trips, so the net effect on $/hour has to come out of the simulation, not be assumed.
- R05 Demand x R10: trip length and origin-destination mix set how much of the shift is spent in slow zones; slow speeds reduce effective supply, which lengthens waits and raises surge (R06, R07).
- R06 Matching x R10: pickup ETA from this engine (R10-050) sets matching radius and acceptance; competing vehicles add to hotspot curb queues (R10-040); airport queue (R06) versus empty return (R10-056) is a direct trade-off.
- R07 Fares x R10: time-versus-distance pay (R10-043; the yellow-taxi meter switches to time below 12 mph), tolls and congestion surcharges (who pays decides R10-021), long-haul rules versus routing objective (R10-042).
- R08 Vehicle and energy x R10: stop-and-go traffic raises gas consumption per mile and favours EV regeneration; idling cost and the anti-idling limit (R10-034); charger and depot deadhead (R10-057).
- R09 Driver x R10: home location drives return deadhead (R10-055); breaks need legal places to stop (relief stands, R10-054); fatigue could lower driving speed or raise navigation errors [design].
- R11 Fleet x R10: sending many cars to one hotspot creates curb queues (R10-040); endogenous congestion only matters at very large N (R10-007); a fleet dispatcher needs fast many-to-many travel-time matrices from the same engine.
- R12 Benchmark x R10: traffic-information level, ETA noise, common random numbers and data era are harness settings (R10-044, R10-045, R10-064, R10-067); the 'learn over 10 days, test on day 11' protocol only rewards learning if slow-day shocks are correlated and partly predictable (R10-009).

## Open questions

- Is on_scene_datetime populated for all HVFHV trips, or only for some platforms and accessible vehicles? The data dictionary says accessible vehicles only; this decides whether pickup dwell (R10-035) and en-route time (R10-050) can be calibrated from public data. The TLC file host was blocked here, so this could not be checked. [answered by fact-check: in HVFHV January 2023 it is filled for 100% of Uber and 0.14% of Lyft trips, and Uber dwell heaps at 2.0 min, so dwell can be fitted from Uber trips below 2 min only, P*]
- Which era should the default scenario replay: 2019 (pre-COVID), 2024 (pre-pricing) or 2025+ (post-pricing)? Speeds and demand must come from the same months (R10-064).
- Are 263 taxi zones fine enough? Short intra-zone trips, wrong-side pickups and curb effects need a road graph; OD calibration needs zones. The hybrid engine (R10-059 option c) is recommended but costs more to build.
- Empty-vehicle speeds are unobserved: TLC records only paid trips and, as far as we know, carry no vehicle ID, so shifts cannot be rebuilt [U]. Is it acceptable to apply occupied-trip speeds to deadhead (R10-058)?
- How should the robotaxi be parameterised for NYC, where there is no commercial service (the Aug 2025 Waymo test permit allowed 8 cars with safety drivers and no passengers, and its renewal was uncertain in March 2026)? Borrow SF/LA ratios or treat them as a scenario knob?
- Does the agent choose routes (R10-042) or only destinations? Exposing route choice adds realism (tolls, highways) but enlarges the action space and token cost per step.
- Several [S] numbers conflict across sources and need a primary read: CBD speed gain (15% vs 11% in a later NBER version), Lincoln Tunnel (10% vs 5% faster), speed-camera trigger (10 vs 11 mph over), bus-lane fine schedule (older $115-$150 vs current $50-$250) [fact-check: the camera schedule $50-$250 is current in 19 RCNY 39-18, P*; 'more than 10 mph over' and '11 mph or more' are the same rule, U].
- No public measurement was found within budget for TNC curb dwell, airport pickup access times at JFK/LGA, red-light camera counts or 2024-2026 Midtown speeds; a follow-up search session (or direct TLC data analysis) should fill these.
- Can Google-derived travel times (Congestion Pricing Tracker, Google Maps API) be used under the 'public data only' rule, or should calibration rely solely on TLC and DOT data for licence reasons?
- At what fleet size N does endogenous congestion (R10-007) or hotspot curb queuing (R10-040) need to be switched on?

3. Travel-time noise is wider than the design guess: zone-pair x hour CV median 0.28 (P10 0.20, P90 0.46), larger for short trips (R10-008). The day-level factor range 0.8-1.25 is confirmed (P5-P95 0.82-1.20), with UNGA week and early December the slowest weekdays (R10-009, R10-004).

5. Pickup dwell: on_scene_datetime is filled for 100% of Uber and 0.14% of Lyft trips (Jan 2023), not "accessible vehicles only". Uber dwell median is 0.8 min (mean 1.0) and 17% of trips heap at 2.0-2.2 min, so the field looks censored at the 2-minute wait clock (R10-035, R10-050). Mean request-to-pickup by borough was 4.3-5.5 min in Jan 2023.
