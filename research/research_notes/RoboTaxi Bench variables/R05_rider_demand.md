# R05 Riders and demand: simulation variables for RoboTaxi Bench

Domain R05 of 12. Owner of the full list: Devansh. Researched on 19 Oct 2026 for the "independent variables list" action from the RoboTaxi Bench meeting.

## Summary

Rider demand matters more for profit per hour than anything else in this simulation. Where, when and how many requests appear, where they want to go, and what each trip pays all come from this domain. The base layer can be built from public NYC TLC trip records: yellow, green and high-volume FHV (Uber/Lyft) trips by taxi zone, with request, pickup and drop-off times, fares, tips, driver pay, and shared and wheelchair (WAV) flags. Those records hold only completed trips, though. They contain no cancelled or unserved requests and no cash tips for taxis, so the simulator has to add back latent demand and impute some behaviours rather than replay the data blindly. The biggest effects on earnings are the zone-hour request rate, the destination matrix and how much each destination is worth for the next trip, airport arrival waves and queues, and rider sensitivity to price and to the quoted wait. For taxis the street-hail encounter process also matters a lot. Smaller but realistic effects include no-shows, late riders, boarding time, party size and luggage, WAV and car-seat requests, tips, ratings, cleaning fees, lost items, disputes and, for robotaxis, doors left open. Robotaxis also need their own rider settings: the AV price premium, the share of riders who prefer a driverless car, no tipping, and no driver to help with bags or a wheelchair. Evidence is strongest for trip volumes, wait times, tipping and WAV rules. Price elasticity, wait-time elasticity and cancellation curves rest on a few older studies or on design guesses, so they should be exposed as scenario knobs.

### How to read the tables

- **Variable**: ID, name, then (priority; applies to).
- **Impact**: effect on driver or fleet profit per hour (high / medium / low).
- **Role**: world = outside input; rule = designer-set rule; agent = model decision; state = changes during run; setting = benchmark knob.
- **Agent sees?**: visible / public_or_forecast (public info or forecast) / hidden (simulator only).
- **Source tags**: [P] primary read directly; [P*] primary via a verbatim copy; [S] search snippet or secondary; [U] unverified background knowledge; [design] our suggestion or guess.
- Driver types: human app driver (Uber/Lyft, NYC "HVFHV"), taxi (yellow/green, street hail plus e-hail), robotaxi (Waymo-like). Levels: single car and fleet of N.

## Key interactions with other domains

- **R01 Geography**: intra-zone pickup points (R05-011) need building, curb and foot-traffic layers. Purpose layers need POIs (hotels, hospitals, bars, campuses), and designated pickup areas (R05-041) are map objects. Devansh's placeholder "roads vs foot traffic density" fits here: hail density per street segment (R05-042) should be driven by pedestrian counts.
- **R02 Time and calendar**: base rate (R05-001), purpose mix (R05-012) and every demand layer are functions of hour, weekday, holiday and season. Nightlife peaks at the 4 AM last call [corrected by fact-check: nightlife-zone pickups peak at 00:00-02:00 and taper to 04:00; there is no 4 AM spike in the TLC data], the airport wave comes at 4-8 AM (airport-bound share peaks at 04:00-06:00, volume at 05:00), campus demand follows the academic calendar, and lunch-hour midday trips are a small bump in the business districts.
- **R03 Events and disruptions**: event end times create bursts (R05-003) and return waves (R05-010). Venue pickup zones (R05-041) also matter. Subway outages shift some riders to cars (R05-026), and flight delays move airport waves (R05-065). The with/without event-info prompt test changes the visibility of these from hidden to public_or_forecast.
- **R04 Weather**: rain raises Uber rides about 25% per rainy hour but taxi rides only about 4%, because taxi supply cannot respond (Brodeur & Nield 2018 [S]). So weather multiplies demand by segment and interacts with price elasticity and surge.
- **R06 Competing supply and matching**: observed demand is already net of competitor supply. Platform share (R05-004), multi-homing (R05-023), street-hail matching frictions with increasing returns (R05-042) and airport queues (R05-063) sit on the R05/R06 boundary. Cancellations (R05-022) depend on the ETA that matching produces.
- **R07 Fares, platform rules and regulation**: price elasticity (R05-020) acts on fares set in R07. Cancellation, no-show, wait and cleaning fee amounts are R07 rules. Mandatory destinations and the ban on refusals (R05-036, R05-059), WAV service standards (R05-031), the CBD congestion fee (shifts demand inside the zone), airport fees, and taxi tip-screen defaults (R05-048) also live in R07.
- **R08 Vehicle and energy**: product and vehicle eligibility (XL, WAV, car seat) and luggage capacity. An EV's range constrains long out-of-city trips. For robotaxis: no tips, cleaning downtime and door-closure failures.
- **R09 Driver and agent decisions**: accept/decline uses the request information (R05-036) and destination value (R05-058). The "take me home" destination filter uses R05-060. Ratings feed deactivation risk (R05-051). Safety incidents can end a shift.
- **R10 Traffic**: trip duration (R05-008) and ETAs drive cancellations (R05-022) and conversion (R05-021). Congestion also makes Citi Bike more competitive for short trips (R05-025).
- **R11 Fleet of N cars**: induced demand from good service (R05-068), rebalancing against directional imbalance (R05-009), WAV obligations, and cars of one fleet competing for the same requests.
- **R12 Benchmark settings and data**: latent-demand uplift (R05-066), replay vs synthetic resampling (R05-069), information levels, seeds, and the 10-day train / day-11 test split. Replaying a known historical day lets a model use memorised knowledge.

## Open questions

1. Unserved demand: TLC files hold only completed trips. How large should the latent-demand uplift be, and can it be calibrated from public signals alone (wait-time spikes, surge proxies, congestion)? No public cancel or unfulfilled-request data was found.
2. What do NYC app drivers see at offer time in 2026 (destination, fare, rider rating)? This decides the R05-036 observation schema and needs a current check. [fact-check: the taxi side is settled by 35 RCNY 80-20(a)(4) and (a)(5): no asking the destination before the rider is seated, no refusing an accepted e-hail; app-side practice is still open]
3. HVFHV on_scene_datetime: the dictionary says "accessible vehicles only". We need to check in the Parquet files which licensees fill it, before using it for rider lateness and no-shows. [fact-check: answered for Jan 2023: Uber (HV0003) fills it on 100% of trips, Lyft (HV0005) on ~0.1%; later months not checked. It still covers completed trips only, so no-shows stay unobserved]
4. Yellow cash tips are unrecorded. Should the benchmark impute them (as a scenario knob) or count only card tips, which understates taxi income?
5. The product split (UberX vs XL vs Black) is not public. Should we set an assumption, or keep only one product in v1?
6. Price elasticity (Cohen et al.) comes from 2015 data in four US cities. Wait-time elasticity evidence is thin, and the NYC 37% figure is unverified. Should both be swept as settings in the eval?
7. There is no NYC driverless service yet, only testing. Robotaxi rider settings (AV premium, preference, door-left-open rate) have to be carried over from SF, Phoenix and LA.
8. Persistent rider agents (with tipper type, rating and retention) or independent requests? Persistent riders make the "learn over 10 days, test on day 11" evaluation richer but harder to calibrate.
9. Should the JFK/LGA holding-lot queue be calibrated from the Port Authority dispatch counts, and how should both the yellow lot and the app virtual queue be represented?
10. Current NYC cancellation, no-show and wait fee amounts ($5 vs $10, 2 vs 5 min) are unconfirmed for 2026. R07 should confirm them.

**Key corrections**
1. **Refusal rule citation (R05-036, R05-059).** The rule is 35 RCNY 80-20, not 56-20. Chapter 56 is the Paratransit Drivers chapter. Under 80-20, (a)(1) makes NYC, Westchester, Nassau and Newark Airport mandatory destinations, (a)(4) bans asking the destination before the rider is seated, (a)(5) bans refusing an accepted e-hail, and (b)(8) lets a driver who has operated more than 8 h refuse EWR, Nassau and Westchester.
2. **WAV standard (R05-031).** The 90%-within-10-min standard is in force, not just proposed. 35 RCNY 59B-17(f)(3)(i) was amended effective 6 Mar 2025. The alternative is sending 25% of trips to WAVs under 59B-17(c)(1). In Jan 2023, WAV requests were 0.15% of HVFHV trips, and 6.6% of all trips were served by a WAV.
3. **on_scene_datetime (R05-038, open question 3).** The dictionary calls it "Accessible Vehicles-only", but Uber fills it on 100% of trips and Lyft on about 0.1%. In Uber's completed trips, rider lateness (on-scene to pickup) has a median of 0.8 min and a p90 of 2.0 min, with 0.5% over 5 min. That is much shorter than the earlier guess of a 1-2 min median.
4. **access_a_ride_flag (R05-017).** No trip is flagged in Jan 2023: the field is blank for Uber and 'N' on every Lyft trip. The flag cannot measure paratransit volume.
5. **Out-of-city trips (R05-059).** 4.5% of HVFHV trips end outside NYC or at EWR (average 16.6 mi), and pickups there are almost nil. For yellow it is 0.7%. The earlier figure was 1-3% [U].
6. **Party size (R05-029).** Yellow 2023: 1 rider 75%, 2 riders 15%, 3-4 riders 6%, 5-6 riders 2%. The earlier figures were 70% / 15% / 10% / 2%.
7. **Taxi tips and payment (R05-048, R05-050).** The share of yellow records with no tip was 30% in Oct 2019 and 23% in 2023, not over 40%. Card trips: 95% are tipped, and tips equal about 22% of fare_amount. Card share of trips was 73% (Oct 2019) and 78% (2023), which replaces the undated 53% figure.
8. **Nightlife timing (R05-016, R02 interaction note).** Nightlife-zone pickups peak at 00:00-02:00 and taper to 04:00. There is no spike at 4 AM last call.
9. **Directional imbalance (R05-009).** In busy weekday zone-hours, the drop-off to pickup ratio runs 0.45-1.45 (5th-95th percentile), not 0.3-3.
10. **Daily volumes (R05-001).** Aug 2024 HVFHV volume was about 617k trips/day (19.13M raw rows), not about 580k/day. The busiest zone-hours (up to 1,625 pickups) are East Village at weekend midnight and Midtown Center in the evening.
11. **Airport pickups (R05-062, R05-064).** Apps took 68% of Jan 2023 JFK pickups and 78% of LGA pickups, against yellow taxis. Of JFK pickups, 39% go to Manhattan and 14% leave NYC. Half of yellow JFK pickups use the $70 flat fare, and $70 is confirmed in the data.
12. **Cancellation impact (R05-022).** Lowered from high to medium. At 5-15% of matches and about 2-5 min lost per cancel, partly repaid by fees, the loss is about 1-3% of driver time.
13. **Misattributed evidence (R05-023, R05-068).** Frechette, Lizzeri & Salz (2019) show increasing returns to scale in taxi matching. They do not estimate rider multi-homing or rider retention, so both rows now rest on [design].
14. **Confirmed from data:** Uber holds 73.5% of HVFHV trips. HV trips grew 4.9% from Dec 2024 to Dec 2025. Trip distances: yellow Oct 2019 mean 3.05 mi and median 1.67 mi, with 81% of trips at 5 mi or less and $18 or less; HVFHV mean 4.8 mi and median 2.9 mi. Durations: yellow median 12.8 min; HVFHV median 15.0 min and mean 18.1 min. Request-to-pickup wait in Jan 2023: mean 4.6 min, matching TLC's 4.7 min. App tips: 19-20% of trips are tipped, worth 4.5-4.7% of fare. Zone-hour overdispersion: variance is about 2.6 times Poisson, with a median CV of 15%.
