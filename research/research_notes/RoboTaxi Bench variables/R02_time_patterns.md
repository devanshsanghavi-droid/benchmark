# R02: Time of day, week, season and calendar

Research dossier for **RoboTaxi Bench**, domain R02, 19 Oct 2026. It lists the time and calendar variables the simulator needs so that a model playing a ride-hailing driver (and later a fleet of N cars) faces realistic hour-by-hour, day-by-day and season-by-season conditions in New York City. It extends Devansh's placeholder "Time of day: average time of having lunch etc." into 69 variables in 10 boxes.

**Source tags**
- **[P]** primary source read directly.
- **[P\*]** primary data read through a verbatim copy. Here this means NYC TLC trip-record Parquet files fetched from GitHub LFS copies whose SHA-256 matches an independent catalog of the original TLC CloudFront files (see Appendix). "Own calc" means my own aggregation of those files.
- **[S]** seen only in a search-engine snippet or secondary coverage.
- **[U]** background knowledge not checked in this session. Verify before relying on it.
- **[design]** my own suggestion, inference or calculation.

**Access note.** nyc.gov, mta.info, bls.gov, Wikipedia, NOAA, BTS, Kastle, data.cityofnewyork.us and the TLC CloudFront host were blocked by network policy, and no proxy, reader or mirror service was used. Only GitHub was reachable. The shared web-search budget ran out after four searches, so regulations and studies I could not read are tagged [S] or [U]. To compensate, most quantitative claims here come from my own analysis of 60 million real TLC trips: all of HVFHV (Uber/Lyft) January 2023 (18.48M trips), all twelve months of yellow taxi 2023 (38.3M trips) and yellow January 2024 (2.96M trips), plus monthly row counts for 2019 and 2022 to Aug 2026.

**Applies-to shorthand.** "human app driver" = Uber/Lyft driver (NYC HVFHV); "taxi" = yellow/green cab (street hail plus e-hail); "robotaxi" = Waymo-like car with no human driver; "fleet" = the N-car evaluation level.

## 1. Summary

Time is the key that every other profile in the simulator is looked up by: on an ordinary weekday, citywide app-ride demand swings 7.7-fold from 4,900 trips an hour at 3 am to 37,700 at 6 pm, and yellow-taxi demand swings 27-fold, so the agent's most valuable decision is when to work and where to be at each hour. Profit per hour is not the same as demand: the busiest weekday hours (4-6 pm, about 12 mph) pay the least per engaged hour ($50), while the quiet, airport-heavy 4-6 am hours pay the most ($62-64) but leave the driver waiting longest for the next ride, so the simulator must model speed, trip mix and driver supply, not only trip counts. Day of week, season and holidays shift the whole curve: app rides peak on Saturday and yellow taxis on Thursday, hybrid work leaves the yellow morning peak about 11% lower on Mondays and 19% lower on Fridays than on Tuesday to Thursday, months vary by about ±7% (app) and ±15% (taxi), and legal holidays cut taxi trips to 45-90% of a normal day while traffic runs 20-45% faster. A few dated, public pulses are worth a lot: New Year's night pays about 60% more per engaged hour with double the demand, Broadway let-outs triple Times Square pickups within 15 minutes [corrected by fact-check: re-computed from zone 230, let-outs roughly double pickups within 30 minutes (e.g. Saturday 94 to 186 per 15 min from 16:15 to 16:45) and reach 2-3 times the same slot on a dark Monday], and airport arrival banks peak at 9-11 pm at JFK while departures peak at 4-6 am. Time-of-day rules are confirmed in the data itself: the $1 overnight and $2.50 weekday rush taxi surcharges (switched off on legal holidays) and the $5 JFK rush surcharge; NYC platform log-in limits, lease shifts and time-of-use electricity add further time rules. Several popular intuitions are small in aggregate data: there is no 4 am bar-closing spike (a smooth taper from 1 am), the lunch bump is only about 12% in Midtown [corrected by fact-check: most of that 12% is the late-morning ramp; Midtown/FiDi pickups at 13:30 (700 per 15 min) match the noon peak (696), so the lunch-specific bump is under 5%], darkness after the DST switch has no measurable effect, and Ramadan iftar shows only a 3-5% dip in yellow trips [corrected by fact-check: re-computed 2.4-4.2%], so these stay as low-impact variables for realism. The data also has time traps: naive local timestamps create negative trip durations on the November DST night and phantom hours in March, files arrive about two months late, the yellow 2023 file is missing about 95% of trips from midday 21 Sep to 24 Sep 2023 [added by fact-check], and 2025 is a different regime (yellow trips +19% after congestion pricing). I recommend building every profile on a 168-slot hour-of-week key × day type (weekday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday, holiday, bridge day) × month multiplier, replaying real calendar dates, starting each simulated day at 4-5 am, and treating calendar awareness as an experiment arm alongside event awareness.

### 2.8 Clock oddities

- **DST spring-forward (12 Mar 2023):** the 2 am hour does not exist. The 3 am hour had 4,267 yellow trips against about 2,300 on neighbouring Sundays, because the 2 am crowd now shows up under a 3 am label.
- **DST fall-back (5 Nov 2023):** the 1 am hour occurs twice and logged 8,435 trips against 5,400-6,000 normally. Of these, 795 have zero or negative durations. Separately, 992 trips that began at 1 am on the March night show durations about one hour too long.
- **Darkness test:** after either DST switch, the share of trips at 5-7 pm relative to noon-2 pm moved by only about 1%. In both directions it fell slightly, so the change is noise, not a darkness effect.
- **Ramadan 2023:** relative to 4-5 pm, yellow trips between 6:15 and 7:45 pm (iftar 7:10-7:40 pm) were 3-5% lower than in the weeks before and after [corrected by fact-check: re-computed 2.4-3.0% lower when the spring-recess week is excluded and 3.6-4.2% when it is kept; sunset 19:10 on 23 Mar and 19:40 on 20 Apr confirmed with astral]. The data cannot say whether drivers or riders caused the dip.
- **Sun times, NYC (computed):** sunset ranges from 16:28 (8 Dec) to 20:30 (21 Jun), and daylight from 9.2 to 15.1 hours.

## 4. Key interactions with other domains

Time rarely acts alone. In the simulator it should be the index that other domains' effects are multiplied by.

1. **R01 Geography.** Hotspots move with the clock: Midtown and FiDi in the morning, nightlife zones from 10 pm to 3 am, JFK at 9-11 pm, and airport-bound trips from residential areas at 4-6 am (§2.1, §2.7). Each zone needs its own hour-of-week profile, not one city curve scaled up or down.
2. **R03 Events.** Event *sizes* belong in R03; this domain supplies the *timing*: end-time distributions (R02-051), the theatre let-out schedule (R02-050), the fixed annual calendar (R02-053) and New Year's Eve (R02-036). The with/without event-info experiment should be crossed with the with/without calendar-info arm (R02-005). Otherwise a model that knows "it's Saturday night" gets credit for event knowledge.
3. **R04 Weather.** Weather effects depend on the hour: rain at 6 pm hits a market that is already short of cars, while rain at 3 am hits an empty one. [U: Farber 2015 reports taxi drivers do not supply more hours when it rains even though demand rises.] Seasons set the weather mix: snow from December to March, heat in July and August, hurricanes from June to November. Sun times (R02-063) feed lighting and the visualiser.
4. **R05 Riders.** Trip purpose, and with it price sensitivity, tipping (R02-020) and destination, changes by hour: commute, airport, leisure, late-night safety. Transit gaps at night (R02-052) push riders to cars.
5. **R06 Competing supply.** Competitor supply is the hidden half of hourly profit. Waits by hour (R02-019) are the best public calibration target. Supply has its own time patterns: the taxi shift change (R02-044), the Ramadan iftar dip (R02-039), seasonal and holiday absences (R02-032), and drivers chasing NYE-type surges.
6. **R07 Fares and rules.** Time bands come in several forms: taxi surcharges (R02-054/055), their holiday exemptions (R02-037), congestion-zone toll bands (R02-056), platform log-in limits (R02-059) and hours-of-service caps (R02-060). [U] NYC's utilization-based minimum pay makes the platforms ration driver hours by time of day.
7. **R08 Vehicle and energy.** EV charging should happen in time-of-use troughs (R02-057, R02-062). Cold months cut EV range. Fuel prices have a weekly and seasonal cycle, with summer blend costing more [U].
8. **R09 Driver.** The driver decides working hours (R02-004), break timing (R02-021) and when to go home (R02-022), all limited by fatigue and circadian rhythm. The team's "take me home" feature is a time decision: when to start heading toward home.
9. **R10 Traffic.** Speed by hour, season, school term and holiday (R02-016, R02-029, R02-041, R02-034) sets how many trips fit in an hour. Holidays run 20-45% faster and the September school start runs 20% slower in the morning.
10. **R11 Fleet.** Fleet decisions are timing decisions. Stagger shifts and charging into troughs (R02-062), pre-position for predictable pulses (let-outs, airport banks, NYE), and respect robotaxi operating-hour windows (R02-061).
11. **R12 Benchmark.** Date selection (R02-003), day-type strata (R02-002), the day boundary (R02-068), the tick length (R02-067), DST handling (R02-064/065) and date contamination (R02-066) all decide whether runs are comparable. For the "learn on 10 days, test on day 11" protocol, day 11's type must be either matched to the training days or deliberately different, and the choice must be reported.

## 6. Open questions

1. **Supply by hour is not in public trip data.** Without driver IDs (removed from HVFHV and yellow), true hourly earnings including idle time cannot be computed. Options: calibrate a supply model so that waits by hour match §2.1; use TLC's monthly driver counts and published utilization figures; or use older FOIL data with hashed medallions (2013) to reconstruct taxi shifts.
2. **Ramadan and other supply-side observances** need HVFHV months with on-scene times (Mar-Apr 2023, 2024, 2025) to tell a supply dip (longer waits) from a demand dip (fewer requests). Only January 2023 HVFHV was available here.
3. **Current values of time rules for 2026** need checking against primary pages: taxi surcharges, the airport fee ($1.75 since April 2023 per the data), the CRZ toll level and any scheduled increase, Uber/Lyft log-in limit practice, the TLC fatigue rule and lease caps. [Fact-check: surcharges, lease caps, the fatigue rule and the Aug 2025 lockout rule are now confirmed from rule text (R02-037, R02-045, R02-054, R02-055, R02-059, R02-060); the CRZ amounts and current platform practice remain [S].]
4. **Real dates or synthetic days?** Replaying real dates is realistic and auditable, but a model may recall famous dates. Synthetic "typical Tuesday" days avoid this but lose realism. Perhaps use both, labelled.
5. **Day-11 test design.** Should the test day match the training day types, or be a deliberate out-of-distribution day such as a holiday or DST day? This changes what "learning" means.
6. **Robotaxi time patterns.** There is no public hourly robotaxi demand for NYC. Do robotaxi riders (novelty, safety at night, no tipping) have a different hourly mix? CPUC data may help.
7. **Bar-close and lunch effects at venue level.** Both are small citywide (§2.7, R02-009). Are they larger at specific venues or blocks, and is that worth modelling for v1?
8. **Regime choice.** 2025-26 data reflect congestion pricing and a +19% yellow rebound. Which year should be the calibration base, and should a pre-2025 regime be offered as a robustness test?
9. **Lunch timing.** Devansh's "average time of having lunch": the ATUS distribution of eating times and lunch-break length was not reachable. The ride-data bump is centred about 11:30-12:00, which fits a noon lunch, but this is unverified.

## Appendix: method and reproducibility

- **Files.** Scripts and outputs are in `R02_time_patterns_data/`: `a1_hvfhv.py` (hourly HVFHV profile), `a2_yellow_vs_hv.py` (shift change, yellow hourly, surcharges), `a3_special.py` (NYE, theatre, nightlife, airports, lunch), `a4_yellow_year.py` (holidays, month × hour, DST), `a5_sun_ramadan.py` (sun times, DST darkness test, Ramadan), plus `hvfhv_2023-01_hourly.csv`, `yellow_2023-01_hourly.csv`, `yellow_2023_month_hour.csv` and `tlc_monthly_rows_from_catalog.csv`. Each script takes `<data_dir> [<out_dir>]` and needs `duckdb` and `pandas` (`astral` for a5). Day-of-week counts, the school-week, summer-Friday and NYD-2024 figures came from short ad-hoc DuckDB queries using the same filters.
- **Filters.** Trips are bucketed by pickup time within the file's month. Speed uses trips of 60 s to 3 h and 0.1 to 100 miles. Wait uses `pickup_datetime - request_datetime` (all HVFHV) and `on_scene_datetime` (Uber only, because Lyft leaves it blank). Holiday ratios compare each date with the median of the same weekday within ±28 days, excluding the listed special dates.
- **Caveats.** January is a single, wintry month for HVFHV, so hourly app-ride shapes for summer are not measured here. Yellow data are Manhattan-heavy. Pay per engaged hour ignores idle time and tips. Trip counts measure served demand, not requests that went unserved.

No new variables were added; the one factual gap found (the September 2023 data hole) was added to R02-065. Impact totals after the fact-check: high 23, medium 30, low 16.

**Key corrections**
1. **New data trap.** The yellow 2023 TLC file (its SHA-256 matches TLC's original) is missing about 95% of trips from about 13:00 on 21 Sep to 24 Sep 2023 (4,633-5,732 trips a day against about 105,000). The September 2023 monthly index (0.90) and September's Friday/Thursday ratio (0.90) are artefacts of this gap (R02-027, R02-028, R02-065, §2.4).
2. **Lockouts (R02-059).** 35 RCNY 59D-22(c), effective 1 Aug 2025, requires Uber/Lyft to give 72 hours' notice before restricting a driver's access on a day and to keep a logged-on driver available for at least 16 hours. Hour-by-hour lockouts fit 2023-24 replays only.
3. **Lease caps (R02-045).** Exact caps from 35 RCNY 58-21: $105 per day shift; night $115 (Sun-Tue), $120 (Wed), $129 (Thu-Sat); hybrids +$3; late return up to $25/h.
4. **Hours cap (R02-060).** Confirmed at 10 h passenger time per 24 h and 60 h per Monday-Sunday week, with an 8-hour reset (35 RCNY 80-14(f)).
5. **New Year's night (R02-036, §2.3).** The 'normal Monday' baseline of 348 trips at 1 am could not be reproduced. The other non-holiday Mondays of January 2024 had 425-533, so 1 Jan was about 14-17 times normal, not 21 times.
6. **Lunch (R02-009, summary).** Pickups at 13:30 equal the noon peak, so most of the "+12%" is the late-morning ramp. The lunch-specific bump is under 5%.
7. **Theatre let-out (summary, R02-067).** Let-outs roughly double pickups within 30 minutes (2-3 times a dark Monday), not "triple within 15 minutes", and stay raised for 45-60 minutes, not 15-30.
8. **Sunday airport returns (R02-026).** Weekday late-evening airport shares are as high as Sunday's (5.5-8.2%). The Sunday signal is in volume (about 1,780 against 1,170-1,460 airport pickups an hour).
9. **Impact downgrades.** R02-013 (bar-closing taper) and R02-039 (Ramadan iftar) were lowered from medium to low, in line with the dossier's own summary. The Ramadan dip re-computes at 2.4-4.2%, not 3-5%.
10. **Robotaxi hours (R02-061).** NYC requires a test operator in the driver's seat (34 RCNY 4-17(d)(4)), and Waymo's test permit expired on 31 Mar 2026, so NYC robotaxi hours are a counterfactual setting.
11. **Transit (R02-052).** PATH and the LIRR run all night, so they have no last train. Only Metro-North has an overnight gap [U].
12. **Event durations (R02-051).** MLB games have averaged about 2 h 40 min since the 2023 pitch clock, not about 3 h [U].
13. **Smaller fixes.** R02-010 (hour labels for speed and pay), R02-020 (weekday vs weekend tip incidence; 16-19h), R02-025 (yellow's busiest night is Thursday), R02-029 (the fastest month depends on the hour), R02-033 (667k), R02-034 (holiday speeds +14-45%), R02-041 (recess -3 to -12%; Sept jump confounded with office return), R02-047 (LGA hourly values).
