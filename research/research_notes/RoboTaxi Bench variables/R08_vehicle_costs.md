# R08 Vehicle, energy and operating costs: RoboTaxi Bench variables

Domain owner: R08 researcher. Default city: New York City. Covers a human app driver (Uber/Lyft, NYC HVFHV), a yellow/green taxi, and a robotaxi (Waymo-like), at single-car and fleet level. Source tags: [P] primary read directly, [P*] primary via verbatim copy, [S] search snippet or secondary (here mostly facts carried from sibling dossiers, which give their own sources), [U] unverified background knowledge, [design] our suggestion or calculation.

## Summary

Vehicle and energy costs decide how much of a driver's gross pay is profit, but most of them are fixed per week rather than tied to any one decision, and the benchmark has to keep the two apart. In an illustrative NYC cost stack for a full-time hybrid driver (700 miles in 50 online hours a week; gross about $34 per online hour, from January 2023 HVFHV driver pay of $57 per engaged hour [P] at roughly 59% time with a passenger [S]), vehicle costs take about 25-34% of gross: rental or ownership plus commercial insurance are $6-9 per hour, while energy is only $1-2 per hour (3-6% of gross) [design]. Energy per mile runs from about $0.08 (45-mpg hybrid at $3.40/gal) to $0.15-0.18 (EV on public DC fast chargers at $0.50-0.60/kWh), so in NYC an EV without home charging can cost more per mile in energy than a hybrid; the real EV penalties are range, charging downtime (18-45 minutes per fast charge plus queues) and winter range loss of 20-40% [U]. Tolls are mostly passed to riders (8.2% of 2023 yellow trips and 11-17% of January 2023 Uber and Lyft trips carried tolls, mean $5-10), but the driver pays on the empty return from the 4.5% of app trips that end outside the city or at Newark, where pickups are almost nil [P]. [corrected by fact-check: TLC rules make the rider pay "all necessary tolls to and from the destination" on trips beyond the city (35 RCNY 58-26(f)(2), 59A-23(b)(2)) [P*], and the trip records show it: 100% of January 2023 app drop-offs at Newark and 56-62% of other out-of-city drop-offs carry a toll (median $20-21) even though westbound Hudson crossings are free [P]. The empty return still costs time and energy, but its toll is usually rider-paid; driver-paid tolls are mainly on empty repositioning.] For robotaxis the cost structure flips: no driver wage, but capex of roughly $0.10-0.67 per mile, depot space, depot demand charges, operations staff, remote assistance and self-insurance, with fewer crashes but rare fleet-wide groundings [design/U]. The most consequential design choice here is the cost-accounting basis (marginal only vs including fixed costs), because it shifts profit per hour by roughly 14-28% of gross and changes incentives such as shift length. Evidence caveat: the shared web-search budget was used up before this researcher's first search, and nyc.gov, eia.gov, afdc.energy.gov and AAA were unreachable, so trip-record facts are primary [P] (computed from local TLC Parquet copies), cross-domain facts come from sibling dossiers [S], and most prices, rules and study figures are background knowledge [U] that must be checked before they are hard-coded.

Two lessons follow. First, energy is a small share of gross for hybrids and EVs, so energy-price variables are medium at most, while rental or ownership plus insurance dominate the level of profit. Second, almost all of the decision-relevant cost is per mile and per minute of downtime (energy, wear, tolls on empty legs, charging time), so the agent's skill shows mainly through those, while fixed costs mostly shift the score and the incentive to work longer (see R08-067).

## Key interactions with other domains

- R04 Weather: temperature drives HVAC load (R08-009) and EV range and fast-charge speed (R08-010); rain, snow and road salt drive car-wash frequency (R08-048) and robotaxi sensor cleaning (R08-062); pothole season raises flat-tyre risk (R08-055).
- R10 Traffic: link speeds from R10 set energy per mile through the drive-cycle curve (R08-007) and idle burn in queues (R08-008); stops per mile set brake wear (R08-047); trips to chargers and depots use R10-057; toll-avoiding routes (R10-021) trade minutes against driver-paid tolls (R08-050).
- R01 Geography: fuel and charger POIs (R01-064, R01-065), toll crossings and directions (R01-020, R01-021), relief stands for free parking (R01-031), out-of-city empty returns (R01-059) and depot sites (R01-063) are the places where R08 costs are incurred.
- R09 Driver: the refuel-or-charge action (R09-059) consumes R08 state; charging is best combined with meal breaks; the fatigue crash multiplier (R09-008) triggers R08-056 costs; home location (R01-061) plus home charging access (R08-029) set the starting charge; robotaxi cleaning, depot and remote assistance (R09-044 to R09-049) sit beside R08-061 to R08-066 in the fleet ledger.
- R06 Supply: the background EV share sets charger queues (R08-025); vehicle-class pools (R06-007) depend on the agent's class (R08-002); the licence pause (R06-053) and Green Rides rules (R08-041) cap fleet growth; robotaxi operating limits (R06-058) and recalls (R08-060) remove supply.
- R07 Fares and pay: product tier multipliers pair with service class (R08-002); toll pass-through rules decide who pays (R08-050); EV per-trip incentives (R08-021) and taxi card fees (R08-043) are pay-side items; NYC's minimum pay formula for app drivers is built on estimated vehicle expenses per mile [U], so R08 values should match the vintage of the pay rule.
- R05 Demand: party size (R08-003), luggage (R08-004) and WAV requests (R08-005) filter which offers a car can take; rider preference for EV or robotaxi products links to R08-002.
- R11 Fleet: procurement and mix (R08-069), spare ratio (R08-058), staggered depot charging under demand charges (R08-020), depot rent (R08-064) and operations overhead (R08-063) are fleet-level levers.
- R03 Events: fuel price shocks and rationing (R08-015), protests that bring vandalism (R08-059) and recall or suspension news (R08-060).
- R12 Benchmark settings: the accounting basis (R08-067), cost information level (R08-068) and simple vs itemised cost mode (R08-049) are experiment settings; data-era alignment (fuel prices, tolls and pay rules from the same period as the replayed trips) must match R10-064.

## Open questions

1. Rental, lease and insurance prices for NYC TLC cars are the largest cost items but have no public dataset; can the team run a quick market scan (fleet price lists, driver groups) or use a driver survey such as the TLC or IDG reports to pin them down?
2. Which cost-accounting basis is the headline score (marginal only, plus fixed per hour, or full economic cost)? It changes profit per hour by roughly 14-28% of gross and changes optimal shift length.
3. What are the frequent toll values of exactly $20.00 (about 214k HVFHV trips in Jan 2023), $21.00 and $3.00, and the many tiny Lyft values ($0.10-0.15)? They look like platform pass-through conventions or artefacts and must be understood before tolls are replayed. [fact-check, partly answered [P]: 99.9% of the $20.00 and $21.00 values sit on trips that end at Newark or outside NYC (Uber 191k at $20 and 58k at $21; Lyft 23k at $20). That fits flat return-toll charges under the TLC rule that riders pay tolls "to and from" out-of-city destinations. The $3.00 and tiny Lyft values remain unexplained.]
4. Green Rides percentages and the current (late 2026) TLC licensing status for EVs and WAVs need re-reading on nyc.gov; they decide which vehicles a fleet agent may add. [fact-check: Green Rides is answered (35 RCNY 59D-13(c): 25% in 2026, 40% in 2027, 100% in 2030) [P*]; whether EV-only FHV licences were released in 2026 is still open.]
5. TLC trip records have no vehicle or fuel field, so the EV share of app trips by year must come from the FHV Active vehicle list (no link to trips) or TLC reports; is a fleet-level share enough?
6. No public history of charger occupancy exists; should queues be simulated from an assumed background EV share, or should the scenario use live network APIs only for forward runs?
7. Robotaxi costs (capex, insurance, depot, operations) are unpublished; agree on two or three named cost scenarios (e.g. 2026 Waymo-like, next-gen, Cybercab-like) and run sensitivity rather than one guess.
8. If robotaxis operate outside TLC licensing in NYC, do they pay per-trip congestion charges, the daily CRZ toll, or neither? This changes robotaxi tolls (cross-check with R01-022 and R07).
9. Should multi-week runs model slow-moving states (battery degradation, depreciation, inspections, retirement), or should a run be short enough that these become fixed per-day charges?
10. Exact 2026 toll and fine values (MTA increase of about 6% in Jan 2026, PANYNJ, camera fines) should be read from primary pages before use.

## Research limits

- The web-search budget shared by all 12 researchers was already used up when this researcher started, so no new web searches were possible.
- Direct fetches of nyc.gov, eia.gov, afdc.energy.gov, newsroom.aaa.com and wikipedia.org failed (DNS or proxy refusal). GitHub search API is not available in this session; raw.githubusercontent.com was reachable and used once for the TLC schema.
- Primary evidence therefore comes from TLC trip records already downloaded into the session (HVFHV January 2023; yellow January-December 2023), analysed with DuckDB (scripts r08_hv.py, r08_yellow.py and r08_extra.py, with outputs, in R08_vehicle_costs_data/).
- All [U] values (prices, rules, study figures) are from background knowledge up to 2025-2026 and must be re-read from the named primary source before they are used as simulator constants.
- Fact-check (19 Oct 2026): the TLC rules behind R08-036 to R08-044, R08-050, R08-051 and R08-053 were re-read from a verbatim GitHub export of the Rules of the City of New York [P*], and several were corrected. Prices, insurance premiums, rents and study figures could not be re-read: the search budget was used up and eia.gov, fueleconomy.gov, irs.gov, mta.info, panynj.gov, nyc.gov and NYC Open Data were all blocked. Those values stay [U].
