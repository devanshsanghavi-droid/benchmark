## 1. Summary

At fleet level, profit per car depends less on any one car's choices than on how N cars are spread, charged and kept in service, and on how much the fleet's own cars compete with each other. Two NYC trip replays on GitHub give anchors: with greedy dispatch about 3,000 robotaxis served about 90% of ~150k Manhattan-internal weekday app requests and about 5,000 served all of them, while utilisation per car fell from 57% (1,500 cars) to 28% (6,000). In the same replay, forecast-driven repositioning cut waits by about 8% but raised empty miles per trip by about 87%. Charging is the second big lever: topping up in demand troughs served 3.4 points more riders at 15% lower energy cost than a fixed 20-80% threshold, 24 chargers run this way beat 72 run on the threshold rule, and with no charging policy 4-12% of demand went unserved. The biggest benchmark design choice is the control architecture: one LLM call per car every 5 minutes for 100 cars is about 28,800 calls a day, far beyond the ~$20 run budget, so v1 should be hierarchical, with the model setting zone targets, charging and the active fleet every 5-15 simulated minutes and a coded matcher assigning trips every ~30 s. Other high-impact variables are who dispatches (platform or own app), the decision epoch, own-car herding, uptime, the labour model and shift roster, fixed cost per car-day, the fleet's effect on prices at large N, correlated fleet-wide outages, and whether the score is profit alone or profit with a service floor. New York adds the lack of any legal path for passenger robotaxis in 2026 [corrected by fact-check: not a new 2026 ban; state law requires a hand on the wheel, NYC testing permits expired 31 Mar 2026 and Gov. Hochul withdrew her Feb 2026 pilot proposal, which excluded NYC (Vital City methodology on GitHub, S); NYC's AV rule allows testing only with an operator in the driver's seat (34 RCNY 4-17(d)(4), P*)], the FHV licence pause, wheelchair-access rules, base affiliation, taxi lease caps and garages in western Queens, each with a counterpart elsewhere. Evidence caveat: only GitHub was reachable, so literature figures are unverified [U] and NYC numbers come from simplified third-party simulators [S] whose greedy median wait was 2.4 to 3 times the real operator's [corrected by fact-check: 7.2 min at 6,000 cars and 8.9 min at 3,000 cars against 3.0 min observed; the README's '2.4x' matches the converged 6,000-car figure].

**Counts.** 69 variables in 10 sub-boxes: 17 high, 40 medium, 12 low impact [corrected by fact-check: R11-012 moved from high to medium and R11-021 from low to medium]; 29 core, 34 extended, 6 stretch.

## 2. Recommended fleet set-up for v1

1. **Hierarchical control (R11-009, R11-012).** Every 5-15 simulated minutes, and on flagged events, the model outputs: target idle cars per zone, the number of cars on duty, a charging plan, airport and event allocations, and a small number of per-car overrides. A deterministic matcher assigns requests every 30 s (R11-011). Model cost then grows with simulated time, not with N, and stays within the ~$20 per run budget (R11-014).
2. **Two dispatch modes (R11-010).** In 'Platform fleet' mode, Uber/Lyft-style dispatch matches the cars and the model only positions them and accepts or declines. In 'Own app' mode the fleet serves its own request stream, as a robotaxi operator does. Scoring differs between the two: own-app mode adds a service floor (R11-065).
3. **A scale ladder with baselines (R11-027, R11-067).** Run N = 1, 10 and 100 on the same seeds. Compare against no repositioning, a proportional-LP rebalancer and a trough top-up charging policy. Port the baselines from FleetPy (MIT licence) rather than AMoDeus (GPL-2.0), unless GPL is acceptable.
4. **Background supply in two modes.** Report every fleet score under both fixed and elastic background supply (R06-068), so that a model is not credited just for crowding rivals out when N is large (R11-056).
5. **Calibration.** Fit the simulated request-to-on-scene time distribution to the HVFHV data before comparing fleets. The third-party greedy simulator's median wait was 8.9 min against 3.0 min observed. Simple matrix simulators are pessimistic.
6. **A KPI panel and risk reporting (R11-064, R11-066).** Report mean and spread over seeds, empty-mile share, utilisation, riders served, wait p50 and p90, availability and compute cost.

It also decides whether the fleet moves prices (R11-056). Report fleet scores under fixed and elastic background supply (R06-068). The matcher flag for intra-fleet dispatch is R06-063; the agent-side action set is R11-010. Rival-fleet messaging is R06-065; own-fleet sharing is R11-016.
- **R05 Riders and demand.** Directional imbalance (R05-009) creates the need to rebalance. Better fleet service draws more requests (R05-068, R11-057). Trip records show only served demand (R05-066), so replays understate what a large fleet could capture. The offpeak simulator names this as a limitation.
- **R08 Vehicle, energy and costs.** Battery size, charging power, taper and electricity price feed charger ratio, schedule and queue (R11-034 to R11-041). In the offpeak simulator, the hourly wholesale price signal changed nothing measurable in NYC, because the cheap hours already coincide with low demand. A -5 C week erased the smart policy's cost advantage, which ties to R04 Weather.
- **R10 Traffic and travel time.** Travel-time matrices set repositioning and charger-trip costs. Empty fleet miles add congestion (R10-007), which neither GitHub simulator modelled. Robotaxi freeway limits (R10-023) constrain airport allocation (R11-024).
- **R01 Geography.** Depots and garages (R01-063), charger sites (R01-065), legal waiting spots (R01-032) and the robotaxi geofence (R01-008) are the map layers for depot siting, charging placement and staging capacity (R11-023, R11-037, R11-043). Under 2025 congestion pricing, taxis pay $0.75 and FHVs $1.50 per trip that begins, ends or runs in the zone [corrected by fact-check: amounts per R07-008, S; was U] (R01-022). Whether an empty repositioning car entering the zone pays anything is an open question.
- **R03 Events and R04 Weather.** Events drive pre-positioning (R11-025). Blackouts, festivals that congest cellular links, floods and heavy weather can ground many robotaxis at once (R11-060). Event information on or off is the with/without event awareness test in R11-017.
- **R07 Fares, pay and regulation.** Surge (R07) is the price a large fleet suppresses (R11-056). TLC rules set the licence cap, WAV obligations, lease caps, base affiliation and minimum pay (R11-008, R11-049, R11-051, R11-055, R11-059).
- **R09 Driver and agent decisions.** Single-car versions of fleet decisions live in R09: repositioning (R09-052/053), duty cycle (R09-044), depots (R09-045), cleaning (R09-046/047), remote assistance (R09-048/049), stuck vehicles (R09-050), home location (R09-015) and memory across days (R09-030). R11 adds pooling, queueing and roster effects across N cars.
- **R02 Time.** The shift roster and active fleet size follow the hourly demand curve. Dead hours (R02-014) are the natural charging and maintenance window. The taxi 4-5 pm shift change (R02-044) is a fleet-wide handover cost.
- **R12 Benchmark settings.** Information levels, the token budget, latency handling, seeds, scoring, multi-run variance and the 10-days-then-day-11 learning test all apply to fleet runs. R11-009 to R11-017 and R11-064 to R11-069 are the fleet-specific settings.

## 7. Open questions

1. How would a NYC robotaxi fleet be licensed (an FHV base? a new TLC class?), and would empty repositioning trips into the congestion zone pay a toll, a per-trip charge or nothing?
2. Which control architecture keeps a 100-car run under ~$20? Measure tokens per decision on a cheap open model before fixing the decision epoch (R11-012) and the per-car override quota.
3. Can the matcher reproduce the real HVFHV wait distribution? A third-party greedy simulator had a median wait of 8.9 min against 3.0 min observed. Forward dispatch and better travel times may be needed before fleet results mean anything.
4. Robotaxi uptime, charging hours per day, cleaning cadence and remote-assistance call rates are not public. Which ranges should be fixed, and which swept?
5. Should fleet scores use fixed or elastic background supply and prices, and how do we stop large-N models scoring well just by crowding rivals out?
6. How far can a fleet operator legally direct contractor drivers in NYC, and what compliance rate (R11-018) is realistic?
7. Which baselines come first: proportional LP, MPC or an orchestrated charging rule? Can FleetPy (MIT) modules be ported without bringing in GPL code from AMoDeus?
8. Several numbers need checking now that the web is blocked: Zhang and Pavone (about 8,000 robotaxis for Manhattan taxi demand), Alonso-Mora et al. (about 3,000 four-seat cars for about 98% of NYC taxi demand), Spieser et al. (Singapore with about one third of its vehicles), Fagnant and Kockelman (one shared AV replacing about 11 cars), Bauer et al. 2018 (costs of an automated electric taxi fleet for Manhattan), Revel's current NYC status, the TLC WAV response-time standards, lease caps and inspection cadence. [Fact-check: the TLC WAV standards, lease caps and inspection cadence are now verified in 35 RCNY (R11-045, R11-051, R11-059); the literature figures and Revel's status remain unverified.]
9. Depot land and grid capacity in NYC: is there public data on Con Edison hosting capacity for multi-MW depots?
10. For the visual demo, should the map show the fleet's target distribution against its actual distribution, so that herding is visible?
