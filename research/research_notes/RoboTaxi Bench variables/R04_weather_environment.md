# R04 Weather and environment: RoboTaxi Bench variables

Domain owner: R04 researcher. Default city: New York City. Covers a human app driver (Uber/Lyft, NYC HVFHV), a yellow/green taxi, and a robotaxi (Waymo-like), at single-car and fleet level. Source tags: [P] primary read directly or computed by us from public data, [P*] primary via verbatim copy, [S] search snippet or secondary, [U] unverified background knowledge, [design] / [design guess] our suggestion.

## Summary

Weather reaches profit per hour through three channels that pull in different directions: it raises demand (rain, cold, heat, smoke, transit failures), it slows traffic and raises crash risk (rain, snow, ice, floods), and it changes supply (yellow taxis are capped, app supply responds through surge, robotaxis may pause). Our own analysis of public TLC trip records joined to NOAA Central Park weather shows this clearly: on wet days in January 2023, Uber completed trips rose about 6% and Lyft about 4% while yellow-taxi trips did not change. Uber pickup waits rose about 9% (22-40% in the evening peak on the four heaviest-rain weekdays, without a trend control) while curb dwell did not change. Across 2023, rain and cold shifted the mix toward trips under one mile (+0.5 to +2.5 points) and left tip rates unchanged. Extreme days dominate the tails: the 29 Sep 2023 flash flood (5.48 in) lifted yellow trips 46-63% in the morning peak while cutting average yellow-trip speeds (citywide, mostly Manhattan) about 30% [corrected by fact-check: not a Manhattan-only measure], and a secondary analysis of NYC DOT sensors finds speeds about 5-9% lower in rain but up to 49% lower in heavy snow. For electric cars and robotaxis, temperature is the second big lever (range about 41% lower at 20°F with the heater on, plus slower cold charging). For robotaxis, the weather operating envelope can remove supply entirely: Aurora reported that weather limited its driverless trucks in Texas about 40% of the time in 2025. The benchmark therefore needs hourly weather (daily data dilute the effect: about +6% per wet day here, against about +25% per rainy hour reported by Brodeur and Nield [uncertain: not re-read at source]), a choice between historical replay and generated weather, and an information setting (none, current conditions, public forecast, oracle) that mirrors the team's with/without-events test. Evidence caveat: NOAA, NWS, Open-Meteo, NBER, IZA and fueleconomy.gov were unreachable from this sandbox, and the shared web-search budget was used up before this domain started. Figures therefore rest on our own TLC analysis [P], GitHub copies of NOAA data and documentation [P*], secondary GitHub notebooks and text [S], and background knowledge [U]. A fact-check on 19 Oct 2026 re-ran every analysis script, re-read the GitHub sources, corrected 11 variables and lowered 4 impact ratings; see the Fact-check log at the end.

**61 variables in 9 sub-boxes.** Impact: 14 high, 28 medium, 19 low (after the fact-check; before it was 15 / 30 / 16). Priority: 14 core, 33 extended, 14 stretch.

## Our primary analysis (method and results)

All [P] numbers below come from our own computations on public data in this session (scripts and small outputs in the folder `R04_weather_environment_data/` next to this file). Inputs: TLC yellow trip records for Jan 2023 to Jan 2024 (39.9 million cleaned trips) and HVFHV (Uber HV0003, Lyft HV0005) for Jan 2023 (18.5 million trips), local copies already downloaded by the R02/R03 researchers. Weather came from NOAA GHCN-Daily for Central Park (USW00094728, 2009-01-01 to 2026-07-31), using the copy in toddwschneider/nyc-taxi-data, and from NOAA GSOD 2023 for Central Park (visibility, fog and gust flags), using a GitHub copy in danielgvb/thesis.

- **Yellow 2023 daily regressions** (378 non-holiday days; weekday and month fixed effects; rain bins by daily precipitation: light 0.01-0.24 in, n=60; moderate 0.25-0.99 in, n=50; heavy >=1 in, n=16; snow >=0.1 in, n=7; temperature bands): trips light rain +4.4% (n.s.), moderate -4.0% (n.s.), heavy -11.6% (t=-1.4); average speed heavy rain -0.4 mph (t=-2.2, dry base 12.9 mph); fare per engaged hour heavy rain -$1.4 (t=-2.4, base $72); share of trips under 1 mile +0.5 / +1.2 / +1.4 pp for light / moderate / heavy rain (t=2.7 / 6.2 / 4.2) and +2.5 pp on cold days (t=5.2); card tip share of fare +0.1 to +0.2 pp (n.s., base 22.3%); airport pickup share unchanged.

## Key interactions with other domains

- R01 Geography x R04: flood-prone links (FDR, BQE, parkways) are R01 road attributes that R04 closes (R04-025); pedestrian density decides where rain raises street hails most (R04-007); bridges and elevated roads ice first (R04-015); snow clearing state by borough (R04-014).
- R02 Time x R04: the same rain matters more at 6 pm than at 3 am (R04-004); seasons set the weather mix (R04-054); daylight timing lives in R02-063 and its driving-side effects in R04-035; the night cycle also feeds the visualiser (R04-061).
- R03 Events x R04: weather cancels or delays outdoor events (R04-032); weather-driven subway suspensions and travel bans are disruptions whose objects R03 owns (R03-044 emergencies, R03-046 subway incidents, R03-049 airport disruptions, R03-020/R03-021 event cancellation and weather at let-out); R04 supplies the weather triggers (R04-016, R04-026, R04-031, R04-032). Official alerts belong in the same public information feed as event news (R04-051), so the with/without-information test should cross event information with weather information, and replayed storm days need the R03-064 memorisation guard.
- R05 Riders and demand x R04: rain uplift, onset surge, short-trip shift, mode substitution and willingness to pay (R04-002 to R04-006, R04-045) are multipliers on the R05 demand generator; HVFHV data only show completed trips, so unserved rainy-day demand must be inferred.
- R06 Supply and matching x R04: in our data yellow trips stay flat in rain while app trips rise, so taxi supply is capped and app supply responds through surge; R06-050 (weather supply response) and R06-058 (robotaxi limits) take R04-039 and R04-046 as inputs; pickup ETA inflation (R04-009) is a validation target for the matcher.
- R07 Fares and rules x R04: surge responds to weather-driven shortage (R04-011); the emergency surge cap (R04-012) switches on in declared emergencies; the yellow meter's time component (charged below 12 mph) partly offsets slow rainy traffic, but fare per engaged hour still fell about $1.4 on heavy-rain days.
- R08 Vehicle and energy x R04: R08 owns the cost side of EV range and charging in cold and heat (R08-010) and HVAC energy (R08-009); R04 supplies the hourly temperature and curve evidence (R04-037, R04-038, R04-023). Crash and flood damage (R04-041, R04-043), winter maintenance (R04-018) and post-storm fuel shortages (R04-030) are R08 cost inputs driven by R04 weather.
- R09 Driver x R04: whether to keep working in a storm (R04-047), heat and cold fatigue (R04-048), and log-off thresholds (R04-046); the 'take me home' behaviour should account for bans and floods before they start.
- R10 Traffic x R04: the weather speed-penalty table (R04-008) feeds R10-006, weather crash hazards feed R10-025, flooded and snow-narrowed links change the network, and low-sun glare (R04-036) is a small link-level penalty.
- R11 Fleet x R04: weather is a correlated shock hitting every car at once (storm power outages are one cause of R11-060 fleet-wide outages); a robotaxi fleet can lose all supply when it leaves its weather envelope (R04-039); pre-positioning ahead of forecast rain (R04-053) is a fleet-level skill.
- R12 Benchmark x R04: weather information level (R04-049) is served as issued forecast vintages under R12-015; replay versus synthetic weather and common seeds (R04-055), counterfactual pairs (R04-056) and data resolution (R04-057, R04-058) are harness settings; replaying famous storm days risks rewarding memorisation, so dates may need to be hidden.

## Open questions

1. Hourly calibration: the NOAA LCD and Open-Meteo hosts were blocked, so all our TLC weather effects are daily. Re-run the analysis on hourly LCD precipitation to fit the onset curve (R04-003) and intensity bins (R04-001/002) before hard-coding multipliers.
2. Unserved demand: HVFHV files hold completed trips, not requests, so rainy-hour demand that went unserved (or was priced out by surge) is invisible. Should the demand uplift be calibrated to completed trips plus an assumed unserved share, or to Brodeur and Nield's published estimates?
3. Snow evidence is thin: 2023 and early 2024 were nearly snowless in NYC. The calibration should add big-snow days (1 Feb 2021 14.8 in; 23 Jan 2016 27.3 in; 25 Jan and 22-23 Feb 2026) once those TLC months are downloaded.
4. Robotaxi weather envelope for NYC: no operator publishes thresholds, and the only hard number found (Aurora, about 40% weather-limited time) is for trucks in Texas. Which robotaxi 'generation' should the default scenario use?
5. Fair public forecasts: archived forecasts at real lead times exist only from early 2024 (Open-Meteo Previous Runs; GFS 2 m temperature from March 2021 and JMA models from 2018). For 2019-2023 replays, should we use NOAA NBM/NDFD archives, IEM text products, or synthetic forecast noise? Which counts as 'public information' under the benchmark rule?
6. Memorisation: replaying famous days (Ida, Sandy, the 2015 blizzard bust) may reward models that remember them. Should scenario dates be hidden or weather be re-timed?
7. Spatial weather: is one Central Park station enough? Its wind sensor is sheltered (mean 5.3 mph), its GSOD thunder flag recorded 0 days in 2023, and summer storms are local; LGA/JFK or radar may be needed.
8. Not verified at source (host blocked or search budget exhausted): Brodeur and Nield's +25%/+4%; Farber's rain-specific finding; AAA/fueleconomy.gov EV and HVAC figures; the details of the Uber 2014 surge cap; 2015 and 2016 travel bans; NWS heat-advisory criteria; FloodNet data access. (Fact-check of 19 Oct 2026: Aurora's ~40% figure is now confirmed verbatim [P*]; the existence of the Uber surge-cap agreement is confirmed in a news sentence [S]; the rest remain open.)
9. Do riders choose robotaxis differently in bad weather (a dry, enclosed car vs distrust of AV performance in rain)? No evidence found.
10. How much of the rainy-day speed drop is caused by the extra ride-hail vehicles themselves (endogenous congestion, R10-007) rather than by the weather?

**Key corrections.**
1. **R04-041 crash multiplier.** The notebook output shows rainy-hour crash rates of -1% to +5% from 08:00 to 17:00, about +10% at 00:00, 07:00 and 18:00, and +20% to +34% at most night hours. The dossier had said "10-35% higher" at every hour. Impact lowered from medium to low, because crashes are rare per driver-hour.
2. **R04-011 surge response.** Impact lowered from high to medium. The measured daily responses are only +2% fare per mile and +0.8% driver pay per engaged hour (weekday controls only), and the hourly surge peaks are unmeasured.
3. **R04-005 short-trip shift and R04-006 mode substitution.** Both lowered from medium to low. The first is a shift of 0.5-2.5 points in the trip mix with no significant change in fare per trip. The second is only a calibration check on R04-002.
4. **R04-002 rain uplift.** The "+10-30% per rainy hour" figure was tagged [S range] and described as requests. It is now tagged as a design range: no source gives it, and Brodeur & Nield count completed rides. Their +25% / +4% figures are still not re-read at source.
5. **R04-004 and R04-009.** The Uber hour-by-hour comparisons (+14% to +25% midday and evening; +22% to +40% evening waits) use the 4 Tue-Thu days with at least 0.4 in, not the at-least-0.10 in definition, and have no trend control.
