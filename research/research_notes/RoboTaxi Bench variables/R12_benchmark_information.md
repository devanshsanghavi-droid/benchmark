# R12 Benchmark settings, information and data: RoboTaxi Bench variables

Domain owner: R12 researcher. Written 19 Oct 2026. Default city: New York City. Covers a human app driver (Uber/Lyft, NYC HVFHV), a yellow/green taxi and a robotaxi (Waymo-like), at single-car and fleet level. This domain holds the experiment's own independent variables: what is being tested, what the agent may know and do, how runs are seeded, scored and paid for, and what the data pipeline and live map need.

Source tags: [P] primary read directly (including our own computation on official data files), [P*] primary via a verbatim copy or saved capture, [S] search snippet, GitHub copy of metadata, or secondary only, [U] unverified background knowledge, [design] our suggestion. 'via D10/D11' means the fact was collected and fact-checked in the team's earlier business-simulation dossiers (research/research_notes/Business sim deep dive/).

## Summary

RoboTaxi Bench measures a model, but the number it reports also depends on many experiment settings that are easy to leave implicit: which public information the agent may query, how often it is called, whether time stops while it thinks, which days it is tested on, whether demand is replayed or generated, how runs are paired and normalised, and how much each run may cost. The biggest levers on the measured profit are the model itself, the information level, the action space and decision cadence, the scenario and day-type mix, the data era, and the scoring rules (floor, ceiling and aggregation). Our own analysis of the official TLC January 2023 files shows that a ten-day learning window contains most of the learnable weekday pattern (predicting day 11 zone-hour pickups from the six prior non-holiday weekdays gives WAPE 0.134, against a 0.120 floor with 19 weekdays [corrected by fact-check: 0.120 is the error of a plain mean of 19 other January weekdays, including days after the target; it is an empirical reference, not a proven floor, and predictors that use weather, events or trend may beat it]) but holds only 2-3 examples for a weekend, and that naive use of all ten days (including New Year holidays) nearly doubles the error (0.238). Citywide app-driver pay per engaged hour is very stable across weekdays (about $54, CV 1.4%) while daily trip volume swings 12%, so models must face identical days (common random numbers) and be compared by paired differences, or day luck will swamp real gaps. Leakage is a first-order risk: NYC taxi data is among the most reused public datasets, frontier models can recall famous tables verbatim, and the TLC publishes each month about two months late, so the 'public at decision time' rule must exclude the replayed day's own trip records and realised weather, forecasts must be served as issued, and test months should post-date model training cutoffs. Run cost is driven by calls per simulated day times context size: the team's $20 per run buys a 10-day run only at a lean cadence (about 40 calls of 8k tokens per day: about $7 on Opus 5.5, $17 on Fable 5.1), while a standard cadence costs about $31-73, so cadence, horizon and budget must be decided together. We recommend a frozen-harness headline with paused sim time, net profit per online hour normalised between a passive floor and a hindsight oracle, aggregated by interquartile mean with bootstrap intervals, plus panels for risk, compliance, efficiency, information value and cost; the live map needs a per-vehicle path-and-timestamp log with model colours, which deck.gl's TripsLayer already animates for NYC (without day/night lighting). Evidence caveat: the shared web-search budget was already used up when this domain started and nyc.gov, the NYC and NY open-data portals, eia.gov, afdc.energy.gov, weather.gov and arxiv.org were unreachable, so dataset identifiers were confirmed only through GitHub copies [S] and most licence terms are [U].

**72 variables in 10 sub-boxes.** Impact: 26 high, 37 medium, 9 low [corrected by fact-check: now 24 high, 38 medium, 10 low after R12-051 and R12-052 moved from high to medium and R12-055 from medium to low]. Priority: 47 core, 23 extended, 2 stretch. All are role 'setting' or 'rule', because this domain is the experiment's own design; world, agent and state variables live in R01-R11.

## New primary analysis: how learnable is day 11, and how much do days differ?

Script: scratchpad r12scripts/r12_learn.py (DuckDB over the official Parquet files; output in r12_learn_out.txt). Unit: pickups per TLC taxi zone x hour. WAPE = sum of absolute errors / sum of actual pickups (lower is better). Top-10 overlap = share of each hour's ten busiest zones that the predictor also ranks in its top ten. [verified by fact-check, 19 Oct 2026: SHA-256 of both local Parquet files recomputed and equal to the catalog checksums (474 MB, 18,479,031 rows; 48 MB, 3,066,766 rows); every number in the two tables and the rolling ranges below matches r12_learn_out.txt; the 20 weekdays exclude 2 and 16 Jan; HVFHV trips with trip_time 0 are dropped, and the day-to-day table also drops trips over 4 h]

**Train on 1-10 Jan 2023, test on Wednesday 11 Jan 2023 [P]**

| Predictor of day-11 zone-hour pickups | HVFHV WAPE | HVFHV top-10 overlap | Yellow WAPE | Yellow top-10 overlap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean of all 10 days (includes the 1-2 Jan holidays and a weekend) | 0.238 | 72% | 0.298 | 76% |
| Mean of the 6 non-holiday weekdays in days 1-10 | 0.134 | 82% | 0.186 | 84% |
| Same weekday one week earlier (4 Jan) | 0.181 | 75% | 0.230 | 81% |
| Mean of 19 other January weekdays (reference with far more data) | 0.120 | 88% | 0.138 | 84% |

Rolling version (each target day from 11 to 31 Jan, except the 16 Jan holiday, predicted from the same day type within its previous 10 days): median WAPE 0.174 for HVFHV and 0.198 for yellow; with all 10 previous days regardless of type: 0.299 and 0.281. Weekend targets have only 2-3 same-type days in a 10-day window. The correlation between zone-hour HVFHV pay per engaged hour on the six training weekdays and on day 11 is 0.84 (5,528 zone-hours with enough trips).

**Day-to-day variation, non-holiday weekdays in January 2023 (n = 20) [P]**

| Measure | HVFHV (Uber/Lyft) | Yellow taxi |
|---|---|---|
| Trips per day: mean (CV) | 576,253 (12.4%) | 102,013 (9.5%) |
| Money per engaged hour: mean (CV, range) | driver_pay $54.04 (1.4%, $52.42-55.82) | total_amount $109.84 (3.4%, $101.88-116.46); gross incl. surcharges, tolls and tips, not driver take-home [corrected by fact-check: total_amount includes card tips only; cash tips are not recorded (S, GitHub copies of the TLC data dictionary)] |
| Median request-to-pickup time | 3.52-4.18 min | n/a (no request time) |
| Holiday contrast | 1 Jan 2023: $72.15 per engaged hour, 6.05 min median wait | |

What this means for the benchmark:
- Ten days are enough to learn the ordinary weekday pattern if the learner conditions on day type, so a 10-day / day-11 protocol is a fair learning test on weekdays; it is not a fair test on weekends, holidays or event days unless the window is longer or includes matched days (R12-035, R12-036).
- About 0.12-0.13 zone-hour WAPE is irreducible day-to-day noise. A learner cannot beat it, so a simulator validated to that level is as realistic as the real data allows (R12-066), and notes that 'predict' better than that on replayed days are memorising (R12-037). [corrected by fact-check: overstated. 0.120 is what a plain mean of 19 other weekdays (some after the target, so not available to a real learner) achieves on one test day; it is a practical reference level, not a proven lower bound. Predictors that use weather, events or trend could beat it somewhat, so treat accuracy far below about 0.12 on replayed days as a memorisation warning sign, not proof.]
- Note from fact-check: 'engaged hour' in this section means trip_time, from pickup to drop-off with the passenger on board (P3). It excludes the drive to the pickup (P2), so pay per (P2 + P3) hour is lower than $54.04.
- Because pay per engaged hour hardly moves while volume moves a lot, profit per online hour is mainly a measure of utilisation and positioning, and day choice dominates raw scores; hence common random numbers, paired comparisons and per-scenario normalisation (R12-040, R12-057).

## Run-cost arithmetic [design, prices P]

Cost per simulated day for one car, assuming 85% of input tokens are prompt-cache reads and the rest are 5-minute cache writes. Prices per million tokens (write / read / output): Fable 5.1 $12.50 / $0.25 / $50; Opus 5.5 $5 / $0.20 / $20; Sonnet 5.5 $2.50 / $0.20 / $10 (Anthropic pricing, captured 30 Sep 2026 and cached 6 Oct 2026). Haiku 5.5 is $0.10 in / $0.50 out; its cache prices were assumed at 1.25x and 0.1x input.

| Cadence | Calls per day | Tokens per call (in / out) | Fable 5.1 per day (10 days) | Opus 5.5 per day (10 days) | Sonnet 5.5 per day (10 days) | Haiku 5.5 per day (10 days) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lean | 40 | 8k / 0.5k | $1.67 ($17) | $0.69 ($7) | $0.37 ($4) | $0.02 (<$1) |
| Standard | 80 | 20k / 1k | $7.34 ($73) | $3.07 ($31) | $1.67 ($17) | $0.08 ($1) |
| Heavy | 150 | 40k / 3k | $35.03 ($350) | $14.52 ($145) | $7.77 ($78) | $0.39 ($4) |

The team's $10k estimate (5 models x 100 runs x $20) therefore assumes a lean cadence or short horizons for the most expensive models. The full learning protocol (100 runs x 11 sim-days) at the standard cadence would cost about $3.4k per model on Opus 5.5 and about $8k on Fable 5.1. A fleet with one call per car multiplies everything by N, so fleet mode needs a dispatcher or policy-parameter interface (R12-030).

[verified by fact-check: all list prices match the bundled Claude API reference (cached 6 Oct 2026): Fable 5.1 $10 / $50 with cache reads $0.25; Opus 5.5 $4 / $20, reads $0.20; Sonnet 5.5 $2 / $10, reads $0.20; Haiku 5.5 $0.10 / $0.50; 5-minute cache writes cost 1.25x input. Every cell of the table and the $3.4k / $8k campaign figures were recomputed and match. Caveats: Haiku 5.5's price rises fivefold for prompts above 100K tokens, and the table assumes the same token count for the same text on every model, while Haiku 5.5's tokenizer counts about 30% more tokens.]

## Key interactions with other domains

- **R01 Geography x R12:** the spatial unit (R01-009) sets the action granularity (R12-025), the coordinate synthesis needed because TLC data has zone IDs only (R12-067), and what map knowledge the harness hands over (R12-018). An anonymised or mirrored city (R12-044) must keep R01's geography intact while hiding names.
- **R02 Time x R12:** calendar flags (holidays, weekdays, seasons) define the training window, the day-11 test strata and the scenario mix (R12-035, R12-036, R12-042). Our analysis shows holidays inside a 10-day window nearly double the error of a naive learner.
- **R03 Events x R12:** the event-awareness arm (R12-008) and the event calendar (R12-016) are the team's headline information experiment. Event attendance and outcomes must be served as known before the event (R12-022), and big-event days form their own test stratum (R12-042, R12-061).
- **R04 Weather x R12:** forecasts must be served as issued, from archived vintages, not as realised weather (R12-015). Bad-weather days are a test stratum, and a model that knows the real date may recall the weather (R12-044).
- **R05 Riders and demand x R12:** replay vs synthetic demand (R05-069, R12-039) and demand censoring (R05-066) decide what the generator can claim. The offer content (R05-036) is part of the observation (R12-023).
- **R06 Competing supply x R12:** the number of competing model fleets (R06-060) is the arena mode here (R12-049). The offer timeout (R06-024) is what makes latency matter (R12-027). Background bots double as baselines (R12-050, R12-051), and surge and queue signals are an information choice (R12-019).
- **R07 Fares, pay and rules x R12:** fares and fees feed the headline ledger (R12-056). TLC rules feed the compliance panel (R12-059), and the role framing picks the rule pack (R12-011).
- **R08 Vehicle, energy and costs x R12:** all costs enter the net-profit headline (R12-056). EV range and charging raise difficulty (R12-047), and the EIA, AFDC and electricity data come through the pipeline (data table below).
- **R09 Driver x R12:** persona, home and value of time (R09-004, R09-020, R09-023) set the role framing and the go-home rule (R12-011, R12-032). Memory (R09-030) and information gathering (R09-067) are governed by R12-033, R12-034 and R12-028.
- **R10 Traffic x R12:** the traffic-information level (R10-045) should be crossed with the information ladder (R12-013). Data era (R10-064) and common random numbers (R10-067) are shared settings, and the travel-time engine provides the map tools (R12-018).
- **R11 Fleet x R12:** the fleet control interface (R12-030) sets cost (one call per car multiplies cost by N). OR planners and the hindsight oracle need fleet versions (min-cost flow; R12-052, R12-053).

## Open questions

1. What exactly does '10 days x 100 runs' mean: 100 independent 10-day runs with fresh memory, or 100 passes over the same 10 days with accumulating notes? The second rewards memorising replayed requests unless demand is redrawn each pass (R12-037).
2. Is the day-11 test meant to be the next calendar day or a harder unseen day type? Our analysis says a 10-day window teaches weekdays well but weekends, holidays and event days poorly.
3. Budget: $20 per run fits only a lean cadence (about 40 calls a day) for a 10-day run at Opus/Fable prices. Should the headline use a shorter horizon, a lean cadence, or a larger budget? Is the $10k total firm?
4. Should the headline use real NYC names and dates, which credits legitimate public world knowledge, or a masked or mirrored city, which blocks recall of specific days? Proposal: real names with masked dates for the headline, plus an anonymised track.
5. Is the driver-app surge heatmap 'public'? Every driver sees it but it is platform data. Proposal: allowed for app-driver and robotaxi roles because a real driver has it at decision time; it is generated by the simulator, not taken from any platform.
6. Paused time or latency-charged time for the headline? Paused is fairer to slow reasoning models; latency-charged is more realistic for ride offers that expire in 10-15 s.
7. What is the minimum effect size we want to detect (for example 3% profit per hour)? That sets runs per model by power analysis after a pilot on a dev model.
8. Which archive gives forecast vintages for NYC back to 2019 (NCEI NDFD archive, Open-Meteo's historical-forecast products)? Not verifiable from this sandbox.
9. Licences: do NYC Open Data and TLC terms allow redistributing derived demand files, and does OSM's share-alike clause apply to a shipped road graph with calibrated speeds? Portals were blocked here.
10. Human baseline: can we recruit real NYC drivers to play the interface, and how should they be paid? Is the Chicago taxi table (with taxi IDs) a usable source of real per-shift earnings for a human reference?
11. TLC records hold completed trips only: how much latent-demand uplift (R05-066) should the generator add, and how is that validated?
12. The existing 'Rideshare Bench' by the Malaysian developer could not be found by GitHub search; please share the link so its settings and gaps can be checked.

3. R12-028: the ProsusAI vending-bench charges 5-75 sim-minutes per tool call, not 5-45.

6. R12-059: the HVFHS cruising cap (35 RCNY 59D-21) binds app companies, not drivers, and was reported annulled in court in Dec 2019 [U]. It is a fleet or robotaxi scenario toggle only, not a driver compliance item.
7. R12-068 and the data table: NREL is now the National Laboratory of the Rockies. The AFDC stations API moved to developer.nlr.gov, and developer.nrel.gov stopped resolving on 29 May 2026. Open-Meteo's free API is for non-commercial use only.

9. R12-016 and the data table: the NYC Permitted Event feed (tvpp-9vvx) lists approved events 'within the next month'. It has 13 columns, including community board and police precinct. Back-tests need bkfu-528j. Big ticketed events on private property generally need venue schedules.
