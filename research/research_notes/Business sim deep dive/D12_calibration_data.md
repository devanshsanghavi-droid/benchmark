## 1. Summary

1. **No open café or vending dataset matches the benchmark.** The best public café data (Maven) is *fictitious* [S via D02]. The best real vending micro-data I found is a CC0 Kaggle log from one coffee vending machine: 2,838 sales over 328 days [P, D]. Supermarket panels (M5, Complete Journey, Rossmann, Instacart, Online Retail II) calibrate demand *shapes*, not café or vending *levels*.
2. **That real log gives concrete stylised facts** [D]: variance/mean ≈ 2.5; lag-1 autocorrelation ≈ 0.28; the top 2 of 8 products give 47% of units; 61% of cards appear once, yet repeat cards make 75% of card sales; prices change about 3 times a year; 97% of payments are by card.
3. **Cost-structure anchors are survivor-biased averages** [S via D05]. NRA limited-service restaurants: F&B 32.4%, labour 31.7%, occupancy 5.2% [uncertain: occupancy figure and its urban/rural split not found in any reachable relay; D05's own check left it unverified], pre-tax 4.0%. ATO coffee shops: cost of sales 33–42% [uncertain: ato.gov.au unreachable and no mirror found, here or in D05]. Use them as *target bands for competent scripted operators*, not as inputs.
4. **Survival and liquidity are validation targets.** BLS: ≈78% of new establishments survive year 1 and ≈35% reach 10 years (each figure is a different cohort). Restaurant year-one failure is 17–26% [uncertain: Luo & Stark and Parsa not re-checked; hosts blocked]. Median cash buffer is 27 days (restaurants 16) [S via D05] (JPMC data are from 2015, and they describe operating firms, not start-ups).
5. **Existing sims are explicitly uncalibrated.** Prosus is "not fitted to actual POS sales" [P]. VB1 elasticities are generated by GPT-4o, and VB2 "keeps the same sales simulation" that "can be gamed" [P\*]. Only RetailSynth (KS-distance fit to Complete Journey) and E-CommerceBench (6,886 desensitised real products) touch real data [P].
6. **Sim-to-real anchors are thin and mostly private.** Project Vend 2 implies a ≈$196/day revenue target [P, D] [corrected by fact-check: was presented as a shop revenue target. It is the LLM "CEO" agent's self-set target in an internal message ("$408.75 revenue (208% of target)"), not realised revenue or an operator figure, so it is not real-world demand evidence; anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2]. Andon Café sold 326 of 1,331 pastries [S] [uncertain: the pastry counts appear in no reachable source; D01's fact-check also left them unverified]. VB2's "good strategy" of ≈$63k/yr from one machine [P\*] looks far above real single-machine economics [U] and must be checked before we inherit it. [fact-check: supported by this dossier's own data. The real coffee machine in §2.2 took ≈276 UAH/day (about US$7/day at 2024 rates), and the Prosus scripted bot grosses ≈€14.9k per machine-year (§2.1). VB2's $206/day is 1–2 orders of magnitude higher]
7. **Calibrate in layers** [design]: fit micro-structure by MLE; match business-level moments with simulation-based inference (method of simulated moments, ABC, `sbi`, BlackBIRDS); keep the *posterior*; draw parameters per seed.
8. **Validate four ways** [design]: a CI suite of stylised-fact assertions over scripted policies (as Prosus requires on PRs [P]); a blinded expert "Turing test"; Morris→Sobol sensitivity (SALib [P]) with *rank stability* as the key output; retrodiction of real deployments.
9. **Licensing splits the datasets.** Redistributable: Online Retail II, Open-Meteo, Meteostat and Melbourne footfall (all CC BY 4.0), plus the coffee-vending log (CC0). Not redistributable: Kaggle competition data, Instacart, Advan/SafeGraph. Ship *fitted parameters*, not raw data.
10. **Partner POS data brings privacy duties.** Card and loyalty IDs are pseudonymous personal data. Aggregate at source, or release synthetic data only.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 What the existing simulators calibrate (and do not)

| Simulator | Real data used | Calibration evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Vending-Bench 1** (Backlund & Petersson 2025) [P\*] | None. "GPT-4o generates and caches three values per item: price elasticity, reference price, and base sales", then applies day-of-week, month and weather multipliers, a variety penalty capped at 50%, and noise | A small configuration sensitivity test (GPT-4o mini only, 5 runs per setting): starting balance $100/$500/$2,500, daily fee $0/$2/$5. A $0 fee did *not* raise sales (the model "appears to get stuck in loops") [corrected by fact-check: quote was "gets stuck in loops"; VB1 §3.5.1]. | **Vending-Bench 2** [P\*] | None new: "keeps the same sales simulation" | Sales "simulated based on equations that can be gamed". Andon adds that "humans frequently manage to negotiate to get things for free in our real-life vending machines". |
| **ProsusAI vending-bench** [P] | None: "The model remains synthetic; it is not fitted to actual POS sales" | Fixed per-product `base_sales` of 2.0–9.0 units/day, elasticity −0.90 to −1.55 [corrected by fact-check: was "2.0–9.0, −0.90 to −1.55". **Takeaway.** RetailSynth is the only public kernel with a repeatable calibration loop: target distributions → KS distance → parameter search → validation notebooks. It is a good template, but for grocery households, not cafés [design].

### 2.5 Stylised facts the simulator should reproduce (acceptance tests)

Each fact is run as an automated assertion over a ladder of scripted policies (random → naive → heuristic (s,S) → oracle), across many seeds [design].

1. **Count noise.** Daily unit sales per site are over-dispersed (VMR 1.5–4) and positively autocorrelated, with zero-heavy slow movers [D, coffee-vending log; P, M5 Guide "intermittency… lots of zeros"].
2. **Calendar structure.** An office site shows a weekday effect (Friday dip) and holiday collapse; a public site shows weaker day-of-week and stronger month effects [S via D02, D].
3. **Intraday shape** fits the venue: café morning peak; vending spread out [S, D].
4. **Skewed popularity.** The top 20–30% of SKUs carry most volume [D; U for the general Pareto claim].
5. **Repeat concentration.** Most customers are one-off, but most revenue comes from repeat customers [D; Instacart 59% reorders, S via D02].
6. **Weather.** Shocks shift sales about 10% with little catch-up [S via D02].
7. **Price response is finite.** No policy earns unbounded profit by raising prices alone, and category demand is inelastic while item demand is elastic [S via D02].
8. **Stockouts.** A median heuristic policy sees stockouts at about 5–10% of SKU-days. Lost sales are partly substituted [S via D02].
9. **Cost ratios.** A "competent median" café policy lands within NRA/ATO bands: COGS 30–40%, labour 25–35%, occupancy 5–12%, net margin −5% to +10% [S via D05].
10. **Survival.** A population of heterogeneous mediocre policies with realistic starting cash (≈2–4 weeks of costs) gives year-1 survival of about 70–85% and much lower multi-year survival. Failures concentrate in under-capitalised starts [S via D05]. [fact-check flag: JPMC buffer days measure the cash an *operating* firm holds against its *total daily outflows*, not start-up capital. A new café must also fund capex, deposits and opening stock: Andon Café spent most of a $21k+ budget on set-up within weeks (AP). BLS exits also include sales and relocations. Size seed capital separately, and treat the 70–85% band as loose]
11. **Waste.** Perishable over-ordering is punished. An agent that over-buys pastries the way Andon Café did should show visible waste costs [S via D01]. [removed by fact-check: the target "should see sell-through near 25%". It rests on unverified pastry counts (326 of 1,331) from a single failing deployment, so it is not a calibration target. A median competent policy should land near real unsold rates instead; a GitHub relay of ReFED 2024 gives bakery ≈6–7% unsold in grocery, [uncertain]]
12. **Negotiation.** Supplier outcomes are bounded by a kernel floor (E-CommerceBench [P]). Humans "get things for free" in real machines [P\*], which argues for a *small* concession probability, not a jailbreakable LLM.

### 2.6 Calibration procedures

**Layered approach** [design]
1. **Micro-structural fit.** Estimate demand primitives where identification exists:
   - arrival rates, hour and day-of-week profiles, and overdispersion from transaction logs;
   - price coefficients only where prices vary (M5 weekly prices, Complete Journey promotions, the price steps in the coffee-vending log);
   - use MLE or Bayesian GLMs (PyMC-Marketing [P via D02]).
2. **Macro moment-matching.** Some parameters cannot be identified from micro-data: walk-in rates for a new site, supplier concession rates, staff quit hazards. Match moments instead (the cost ratios, survival and buffers in §2.4):
   - method of simulated moments (MSM) / simulated minimum distance (Grazzini & Richiardi 2015 [U]);
   - ABC;
   - neural simulation-based inference: `sbi` offers NPE, NLE and NRE (neural posterior, likelihood and ratio estimation) and is Apache-2.0 [P]; BlackBIRDS offers gradient-assisted inference for differentiable simulators and is MIT [P]. Background: Cranmer, Brehmer & Louppe 2020 [U].
   - Platt (2020) compares economic ABM calibration methods; Lamperti et al. (2018) use ML surrogates to cut simulation cost [U].
3. **Pattern-oriented modelling** (Grimm et al. 2005 [U]). Require *several* patterns at once (§2.5), so that parameters fitted to one pattern cannot hide errors elsewhere. Windrum, Fagiolo & Moneta (2007) list indirect calibration, Werker-Brenner and history-friendly approaches, and warn about over-parameterisation [U].
4. **Model discrepancy.** Following Kennedy & O'Hagan (2001) [U], assume the simulator is wrong by some structured amount. Inflate posteriors rather than over-fit to one dataset.
5. **Domain randomisation.** Draw environment parameters per seed from the calibrated posterior, in the spirit of Tobin et al. 2017 [U]. This addresses realism (uncertainty is honest) and gaming (no single parameter set to memorise) at once.

**Sensitivity analysis** [design]
- Screen 50–200 parameters with Morris, then compute Sobol indices for the score and *for the pairwise ranking of agents*. SALib provides Sobol, Morris, eFAST, Delta, PAWN and Shapley effects (MIT) [P]; see Saltelli et al.'s *Primer* [U].
- For any parameter whose plausible range flips rankings, do one of three things: pin it with data; randomise it and report the marginal; or report results per regime (e.g. "office site" and "street site").
- VB1 shows why this matters [P\*]. A $0 daily fee made GPT-4o mini *worse*, by looping. [corrected by fact-check: was "made GPT-4o mini worse". VB1 §3.5.1 says setting the fee to zero "does not increase sales" because the model "appears to get stuck in loops". It reports no decrease, and the test used 5 runs per setting with no significance test] Configuration interacts with agent behaviour, not just with scores.

### 2.7 Expert review and sim-to-real validation

- **Blinded "Turing test" on traces** [design; technique listed in Sargent's validation taxonomy, U]:
  - Show café owners, vending route operators and a small-business accountant 8–12 weekly P&Ls and daily sales charts, half real (partner data or the coffee-vending log) and half simulated.
  - Measure discrimination accuracy and collect "tells".
  - Re-run after each kernel revision.
- **Face-validity interviews**: ask "+10% on a latte: what happens over 4 weeks?" and record the answers as priors.
- **Retrodiction** [design]:
  - Feed the *real* decisions from Project Vend or Andon Café logs (price changes, orders, discounts) into the simulator, and compare simulated and actual sales, margin and waste.
  - This needs Andon's or Anthropic's logs, which are not public.
  - Without them, use the incident list (below-cost sales, discount-code leakage, hallucinated payment accounts, over-ordering perishables) as a *coverage* test: can the simulator elicit and price each failure? [P, S via D01]
- **Shadow mode / real twin.** A partner operator runs the agent in recommend-only mode, or we run one real machine on the same harness. Compare simulator predictions with realised P&L quarterly.

### 2.8 Licensing and privacy constraints

- **Do not redistribute** Kaggle competition data. Current standard rules permit "non-commercial purposes only" and forbid making data available to non-participants [S]. Instacart is non-commercial [S]. Advan/SafeGraph is licensed [S]. Ship fitted parameter files with provenance hashes instead [design].
- **Attribution.** CC BY sources (Open-Meteo, Meteostat, Online Retail II, Melbourne) need credit lines in the benchmark docs. Open-Meteo also asks for a visible attribution link and limits free API use to non-commercial, under 10k calls/day [P]. Commercial benchmark operation may need a paid plan or a self-hosted copy of the AGPL server [P].
- **Code licences.** RetailSynth, E-CommerceBench and Prosus are Apache-2.0 [P]. Prosus's training-exclusion request is noted in D10.
- **Contamination.** Famous datasets (M5, Rossmann, Online Retail) are likely memorised by frontier models [U]. Replaying real series lets a model recognise the world. Use them to fit generators, then perturb [design].
- **Privacy** [design; legal status U]:
  - POS logs with card or loyalty IDs, staff shift logs and CCTV-derived footfall are personal data under GDPR even when pseudonymised. A Stockholm partner falls under GDPR; CCPA thresholds usually exempt a small US shop [S via D09].
  - Use a data-processing agreement, aggregate to counts at the source, and suppress small cells.
  - Release only synthetic data produced by the calibrated simulator, optionally with differential privacy.
  - Never seed LLM customer personas from identifiable real customers.

## 4. Design implications for the benchmark

**Build**
1. **A calibration package, separate from the kernel** [design]:
   - `targets.yaml`: the stylised facts and bands of §2.5, each with source and tag;
   - `fit/`: notebooks that fit priors on the redistributable datasets;
   - `posterior.npz`: parameter draws;
   - `calibrate.py`: runs the scripted-policy ladder and checks targets.
   - Make it a CI gate on every kernel change, as Prosus does for scripted calibration [P]. [corrected by fact-check: Prosus CI (tests.yml) only checks that the scripted reference *completes* both horizons. Before/after numbers are a PR-template checkbox and a CONTRIBUTING request, reviewed by hand. A band-asserting gate would go beyond Prosus]
2. **Venue presets** (office vending, street vending, neighbourhood café, office café), each with its own intraday, weekly and footfall profiles. The coffee-vending log shows a public site behaves unlike an office [D].
3. **A policy ladder** in which every rung's expected band is documented. Andon's "$63k good strategy" [P\*] and Prosus's €61k scripted bot [P] are examples. [corrected by fact-check: the two are not comparable. VB2's $63k is one machine over 302 days. Prosus's €61,219 is the mean final balance of a **six-machine** network over 365 days, about €10k per machine] Our ceiling estimate should be checked against real-world revenue per site, not only internal optima.
4. **Two kinds of numbers in reports**: score (net worth) and *realism diagnostics per run*: cost ratios, sell-through, stockout rate, price-change frequency. Use these to catch runs that score by exploiting unrealistic dynamics.
5. **Private test parameters**: public dev posterior, private test draws. Rotate per release.

**Avoid**
- LLM-generated demand parameters, above all when conditioned on the agent's own price or stock (VB1 and open-vending-bench pattern) [P\*, P via D02]. (VB1 has GPT-4o generate the parameters; the paper does not say they are conditioned on the agent's price or stock. Per D02, that conditioning is open-vending-bench's.)
- Constant elasticity with abs(e) < 1, uncapped demand, or no finite customer population. Together these permit the "sell $500 tungsten cubes forever" ceiling [P\*]. [corrected by fact-check: the quoted phrase is not in the source. VB2 says its *real-life* machines "sell tungsten cubes for $500", and it attributes the simulated ceiling to unbounded high-value sourcing, jailbreakable suppliers and gameable sales equations. It does not cite constant elasticity, and VB1's impact factor works from the percentage gap to the reference price. The elasticity point is this dossier's inference]
- Replaying famous public series verbatim (memorisation), and fictitious datasets (Maven) presented as ground truth.
- Survivor-biased averages used as *inputs*. NRA and ATO figures describe operating businesses; use them as targets for competent operators.
- Mixing currencies and years without an index.

**Known exploits tied to calibration**
- **Gameable sales equations** (VB2) [P\*]: deterministic multipliers plus small noise let an agent find a fixed optimal configuration after 60 days. Fix: latent persistent demand states, a finite customer pool and posterior randomisation.
- **Jailbreakable supplier prices** [P\*]: a kernel floor plus a small calibrated concession probability.
- **Unbounded product space** ("items with higher value than… typically found in a vending machine") [P\*]: bound willingness to pay by customer budgets drawn from venue presets.
- **Repricing every day**: if customer fairness is not modelled, daily dynamic pricing extracts surplus that real customers would punish. Use the reference-price adaptation and complaint processes in D02.
