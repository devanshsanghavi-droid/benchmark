# In most US cities, Uber and Lyft drivers see pay, trip time and distance, and drop-off before accepting. NYC is the main exception, and no law there requires any of it

*21 Oct 2026. Based on three fact-checked dossiers (`offer_information/uber.md`, `lyft.md`, `rules.md`). The NYC rules were re-read word for word from the jehiah/nyc_code_archive export, current through rules effective 13 Aug 2026 [P*]. Uber, Lyft, state and news sites were blocked on 21 Oct 2026, so all app-practice claims are search snippets [S] or background knowledge [U]. None could be re-read today.*

**Tags:** [P] = primary source we read directly; [P*] = word-for-word copy of a primary source; [S] = search snippet or secondary source (news, driver blogs, or an official page we saw only as a snippet); [U] = unverified. ⚠ = uncertain.

## Answer first

Yes, in most US markets. Uber since 2022-23 [S] and Lyft since June 2023 [S] show the driver's estimated pay, the pickup time and distance, the trip time and distance, and the drop-off location before the driver taps Accept. The pay excludes tips and tolls.

NYC is the exception:

- **Lyft NYC:** no upfront pay. Since 1 Jul 2024, the destination, time and distance appear only if the driver's acceptance rate is at least 85% [S] ⚠. We cannot confirm this is still in force in 2026.
- **Uber NYC:** we found no source on the NYC offer card. It is probably the standard US card [U] ⚠.
- **NYC law:** nothing requires Uber or Lyft to show drivers anything before they accept [P*].

Drivers never see what the rider pays or the platform's cut before accepting. Colorado may be an exception ⚠. Drivers usually have about 10-15 seconds to respond [S].

**Broadcast offers (R06-026).** Uber's Trip Radar sends an offer to several drivers. A tap of "Match" does not win it by speed; the trip goes to the best-placed driver (US by early 2022, relaunched 15 May 2024) [S].

## After accepting, during and after the trip

- **Pickup.** Navigation to the rider. At pickup: name, an optional PIN, and a destination check. The full route appears after pickup [S].
- **Pay changes.** Uber changes the fare if the destination changes or a long delay occurs. Since Sep 2026 the new fare shows in the app, and a small change that would pay less keeps the old fare [S]. Lyft adjusts pay only for "significant" changes, about 4% of rides [S official].
- **NYC destination changes.** Drivers must follow the rider's new destination unless it is impossible or unsafe (80-16(b)) [P*].
- **Wait and delay pay.** Uber pays a wait fee after 2 min and a stop fee after 1 min (Sep 2026). Its delayed-ride pay excludes NYC [S]. Lyft pays wait time after 1 min (from late 2025) and extra pay when a ride runs 5+ min over its estimate (not in NYC) [S].
- **Back-to-back offers.** Mid-trip offers can be queued. Lyft auto-accepts by default [S official]. Uber's Trip Guide (24 Sep 2026) shows the next pickup, drop-off and earnings on one screen; rollout status unknown [S] ⚠.
- **After the trip.** Tips appear with a delay. Lyft shows the rider payment split per ride and weekly (6 Feb 2024) [S official]. From 1 May 2026, Lyft caps its fee at 30% a month [S official]. In Colorado, one screen shows what the rider paid, the driver's pay, the tip and the take (1 Feb 2025) [S].

## While waiting between trips

- **Heat maps.** Uber's map shows wait times and surge in dollars, built from the last 30 min and updated every 10 min (Rides Heatmap, Oct 2025) [S] ⚠. Lyft shows Turbo and Flash Turbo zones, and from fall 2026 points to the nearest one [S official].
- **Airport queues.** Uber shows a wait band, the number of cars in the queue and the flights due in the next hour [S]. Both apps use first-in-first-out queues where repeated declines lose your place [S]. In NYC, app drivers may not accept dispatches while at the JFK or LGA pickup locations (80-19(f)) [P*].
- **Destination filters.** Both apps allow 2 a day. Uber gives Platinum and Diamond a third [S]. Lyft also has "stay within area" and EV-range filters [S official].
- **Quests and bonuses.** Uber: Quests, Boost+, Reserve. Lyft: Turbo, Ride Challenges, and an AI "Lyft Advisor" (fall 2026) [S].
- **NYC lockouts.** Until 2024, drivers were often blocked from going online "due to high supply" [S]. Since 1 Aug 2025, platforms must give 72 h notice and at least 16 h of access once a driver is online [P*].

## What drivers never see

- What the rider pays, and the platform's cut on that trip, before accepting. Colorado law requires a consumer-charge estimate at offer time; whether it is on the card is unconfirmed ⚠ [S].
- The tip, or the rider's tipping history. Lyft tested showing tipping history and cancelled the test in Oct 2025 after rider backlash [S].
- The rider's last name and phone number [S].
- How pay is calculated (no rate card), and how many other drivers see a Trip Radar offer [S].
- Whether a declined ride is re-offered to others at a different price. One driver saw it happen; Lyft denies auction pricing [S].
- In NYC: why a lockout happened. The rules require notice but not reasons [P*].

## How this changed driver behaviour

- **Visible destinations led to cherry-picking.** Uber says about a third of California drivers declined more than 80% of requests when destinations were shown (2020-21). This is a company claim made during a policy fight [S] ⚠. In India, Uber's 2022 destination pilot *reduced* cancellations [S].
- **Pickup time is the strongest reason to decline.** Surge, long trips and good rider ratings raise acceptance (Ashkrof et al. 2022; a Seattle study) [S].
- **Drivers use rules of thumb.** They decline below about $1.50-2/mi or $0.30-0.50/min, decline long pickups for short trips, decline trips that end far from demand, and run Uber and Lyft at once [U]. NYC Lyft drivers keep acceptance at 85% or more so they can see destinations [U].
- **Upfront pricing coincided with a larger platform cut.** A Columbia study cited by NELP puts Uber's take at about 32% when upfront pricing started and about 42% by end-2024; Uber says about 20% [S].
- **In NYC, time and distance almost fully predict pay.** A fitted time-and-distance formula explains 90% (Uber) and 95% (Lyft) of the variation in per-trip pay (R² 0.90 and 0.95, TLC data, Jan 2023) [P, own calculation]. So a NYC driver who sees time and distance can largely infer the pay.

## What this means for RoboTaxi Bench

**Recommended default observation at offer time (NYC, 2026, "full upfront"):**

| Field | Value | Noise |
|---|---|---|
| `offer_mode` | exclusive or broadcast (R06-026) | — |
| `product` | standard, XL, black, WAV | — |
| `pickup_eta_min`, `pickup_mi` | estimated | The simulated pickup time varies around the estimate; fit the spread from simulated travel times [U] |
| `trip_est_min`, `trip_est_mi` | estimated | The actual trip is drawn from TLC trip-time spreads by zone pair and hour |
| `dropoff_zone` | TLC taxi zone ID, not the exact address | Matches the unclear precision of real apps ⚠ |
| `stops` | count | — |
| `est_driver_pay_usd` | Including surge or boost; excluding tips and tolls; at least the TLC floor | Paid as shown unless the trip changes (redraw about 4% of trips, per Lyft's figure) |
| `est_pay_per_hour` | Optional, derived (Lyft-style) | — |
| `rider_rating` | e.g. 4.6-5.0 | — |
| `timeout_s` | 15 s of simulated time | Model thinking time is not counted |
| `account_state` | acceptance rate over the last 100 offers, lockout status | — |

Keep these hidden: the rider price, the platform take, the tip, the rider's identity, and other drivers' choices.

**Information levels to test (R06-025 setting):**

1. **L0, street hail (taxi).** The agent sees only the pickup location; the destination and fare are revealed after committing [P*].
2. **L1, blind app (before 2022).** The agent sees product, pickup time and distance, and rider rating.
3. **L2, Lyft NYC style.** Destination, time and distance appear only while acceptance is at least 85%. No pay figure; pay follows the time-and-distance formula.
4. **L3, full upfront (default).** The table above.
5. **L4, Colorado style.** L3 plus the rider charge and the take rate.

Comparing L1 and L3 measures how much the agent profits from cherry-picking. L2 tests whether the agent learns to manage its acceptance rate.

**Rules to enforce in every level:**

- Declines count against the acceptance rate.
- A run of declines may trigger a short lockout. In NYC, lockouts must stay under 72 h and 168 h a year, per Admin Code 20-1281 [P*].
- After accepting, the agent may not cancel because of a mandatory destination (80-20(a)(1)) ⚠. It is unsettled whether declining an app offer counts as a "refusal".

**Variables this settles:**

- **R06-025, Upfront offer information:** default L3, with L0-L4 as the settings to test.
- **R07-041, Information shown before accepting:** NYC has no disclosure duty. The Lyft 85% gate and the 80-20 refusal rule apply.
- **R05-036, Request information shown to the driver at offer:** the field list above. Merge R05-036 into R07-041.

**Still open:** the Uber NYC card, whether the Lyft 85% gate is still in force, NYC timeout values, and how precise the destination is. To close them, re-check help.uber.com and help.lyft.com when they are reachable, or ask NYC drivers.
